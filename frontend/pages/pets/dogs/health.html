Clinical Overview

Common Dog Health Problems

Be aware of frequent health issues affecting dogs across breeds.

Skin Infections

Caused by allergies, parasites, or bacterial growth leading to persistent scratching and redness.

Ear Infections

Frequent in floppy-eared dogs; marked by head shaking, odor, and discharge.

Dental Problems

Periodontal disease resulting from tartar accumulation, bad breath, and gum inflammation.

Digestive Issues

Stomach upsets triggered by dietary indiscretion, parasites, or infections.

Allergies

Reactions to environmental triggers like pollen, fleas, or specific food proteins.

Obesity

A preventable condition leading to secondary joint stress, diabetes, and heart complications.

Joint Problems

Degenerative conditions like canine arthritis or hip dysplasia causing stiffness and pain.

Parasites & Worms

Internal worms and external pests that compromise nutritional intake and skin health.

Early Detection

Common Symptoms & Warning Signs

Know when your dog needs professional veterinary intervention.

Loss of Appetite

Refusing meals for over 24 hours often signals underlying systemic illness, pain, or dental distress.

Vomiting

Occasional spitting up can be minor, but frequent or violent vomiting requires prompt veterinary care.

Diarrhea

Watery or frequent stools can cause rapid dehydration and indicate infection or dietary issues.

Excessive Scratching

Constant biting, licking, or scratching points to allergies, fleas, or skin infections.

Unusual Tiredness

Lethargy and reluctance to engage in regular play or walks indicate pain, fever, or organ strain.

Coughing or Sneezing

Persistent hacking, reverse sneezing, or nasal discharge can signal kennel cough or allergies.

Excessive Thirst

Unusual water consumption may point to metabolic issues like diabetes or kidney conditions.

Changes in Behaviour

Sudden aggression, anxiety, hiding, or confusion often stem from discomfort or cognitive aging.

Difficulty Walking

Limping, stiffness upon rising, or reluctance to climb stairs indicate joint or neurological pain.

Immunization

Vaccination Guide

Protecting your dog against preventable infectious diseases.

Core Vaccines

Essential immunizations recommended for all dogs regardless of lifestyle to protect against severe contagions.

Rabies Vaccination

Legally required in most regions; protects against a fatal viral disease transmissible to humans.

DHPP Vaccination

The standard multi-antigen shot covering Distemper, Hepatitis, Parvovirus, and Parainfluenza.

Non-Core Vaccines

Lifestyle-dependent vaccines such as Bordetella (kennel cough) and Leptospirosis based on exposure risk.

Puppy Schedule

Administered in a series starting at 6-8 weeks of age with boosters every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks old.

Adult Boosters

Periodic revaccination intervals (1 to 3 years depending on the vaccine type and veterinary protocol).

⚠️ Note: Fixed vaccination schedules do not apply universally. Exact timing and requirements must always be confirmed with a licensed veterinarian.

Parasite Control

Deworming & Parasite Prevention

Shielding dogs from harmful internal worms and external pests.

Internal Parasites

Roundworms, tapeworms, and hookworms reside in the GI tract, causing weight loss and nutritional deficiencies.

External Parasites

Ticks, fleas, and mites trigger severe skin irritation, dermatitis, and transmit tick-borne diseases.

Importance of Deworming

Routine deworming prevents heavy parasitic burdens and protects both canine and human household members.

Tick & Flea Prevention

Use vet-approved topical spot-ons, chewable tablets, or collars to maintain continuous protection.

Regular Fecal Screening

Periodic fecal examinations at the clinic ensure hidden internal parasites are caught and treated early.

Wellness Exams

Regular Veterinary Checkups

Proactive monitoring is the bedrock of lifelong canine health.

Routine Physical Exams

Comprehensive nose-to-tail wellness evaluations performed at least once or twice a year.

Dental Checkups

Professional inspection of teeth and gums to catch periodontal disease in its early stages.

Weight Monitoring

Tracking body mass index and body condition score to prevent weight-related disorders.

Recommended Blood Tests

Baseline bloodwork and organ function panels, especially recommended for adult and senior dogs.

Preventive Screening

Early diagnostic testing to identify silent conditions before clinical symptoms emerge.

Urgent Response

First Aid & Emergency Care

Knowing what to do when critical situations arise.

Minor Cuts & Wounds

Gently clean with saline solution and apply pet-safe antiseptic. Monitor for signs of infection.

Controlling Bleeding

Apply firm, direct pressure using a clean cloth or sterile gauze pad until bleeding subsides.

Suspected Poisoning

Keep packaging handy and call a vet immediately. Never induce vomiting unless explicitly instructed.

Heatstroke

Move to shade, apply cool (not cold) water to paws and fur, and rush to an emergency clinic immediately.

Choking

If safe, check the mouth to remove visible obstructions. Use modified Heimlich maneuvers for dogs if needed.

Emergency Contacts

Keep your 24/7 local emergency vet hospital number and poison control hotline pinned visibly at home.

⚠️ Disclaimer: First-aid information does not replace professional veterinary treatment. Suspected poisoning, choking, heatstroke, and severe trauma require urgent veterinary attention.

Oral Hygiene

Dental Health

Fresh breath and strong teeth are vital for overall systemic health.

Oral Hygiene Importance

Prevents bacteria from entering the bloodstream and damaging the heart, kidneys, and liver.

Brushing Dog Teeth

Introduce daily or regular toothbrushing using flavored pet-safe toothpaste (never human toothpaste).

Dental Chews & Toys

Provide veterinary-approved dental chews that mechanically reduce tartar buildup during chewing.

Signs of Dental Trouble

Look out for foul breath, yellowish-brown tartar, drooling, or reluctance to chew hard kibble.

Professional Cleaning

Under-anesthesia scaling and polishing performed by a vet to clean beneath the gumline safely.

Psychological Well-being

Mental Health & Emotional Wellness

A healthy mind is just as important as a healthy body for your dog.

Separation Anxiety

Distress when left alone; manage with gradual desensitization and comfort items.

Stress & Fear

Reaction to loud noises or unfamiliar environments; provide safe, quiet retreat spaces.

Mental Stimulation

Use puzzle toys, scent games, and obedience training to keep cognitive functions sharp.

Social Interaction

Positive exposure to other pets and people builds confidence and reduces fear-based reactivity.

Best Practices

Preventive Health Care

Daily habits that keep common illnesses at bay.

  • Regular Health Monitoring: Inspect coat, eyes, ears, and paws weekly.
  • Healthy Weight Maintenance: Measure portions and limit high-calorie treats.
  • Clean Drinking Water: Provide fresh, filtered water in sanitized bowls daily.
  • Hygiene & Sanitation: Regular baths, brushing, and clean living quarters.
  • Parasite Prevention: Consistent year-round flea, tick, and worm protection.
  • Routine Veterinary Visits: Keep up with wellness checks and vaccine boosters.
Golden Years

Senior Dog Health

Specialized care for aging canine companions.

Age-Related Changes

Slowing metabolism, graying muzzle, and decreased stamina require dietary and activity adjustments.

Arthritis & Joint Care

Provide orthopedic bedding, non-slip rugs, and joint supplements recommended by your vet.

Vision & Hearing Decline

Adapt home layouts for dogs experiencing cataracts or hearing loss by keeping furniture in fixed places.

Bi-Annual Vet Checkups

Older dogs benefit from health screenings every 6 months to detect organ decline early.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common dog health inquiries.

How often should I take my dog to the vet? +
Adult dogs should visit at least once a year for a routine wellness exam. Puppies require more frequent visits for vaccines, while senior dogs (7+ years) benefit from bi-annual checkups.
Which vaccines are necessary for dogs? +
Core vaccines, including Rabies and DHPP (Distemper, Hepatitis, Parvovirus, Parainfluenza), are recommended for all dogs. Non-core vaccines depend on your dog's specific lifestyle and geographical exposure.
How often should dogs be dewormed? +
Puppies require frequent deworming schedules. Adult dogs are typically treated every 3 months or based on fecal exam results and lifestyle risk factors.
What are common signs of illness in dogs? +
Common indicators include lethargy, sudden loss of appetite, vomiting, diarrhea, unusual scratching, behavioral shifts, or difficulty rising.
How do I know if my dog has a fever? +
A dog's normal temperature ranges between 101°F and 102.5°F. Signs of fever include warm ears, lethargy, shivering, and watery eyes. A rectal thermometer provides the accurate reading.
What should I do if my dog stops eating? +
If food refusal lasts more than 24 hours or is accompanied by vomiting or lethargy, consult your veterinarian immediately to rule out illness or pain.
How can I prevent ticks and fleas? +
Use veterinarian-recommended preventive treatments such as monthly topical spot-ons, chewable tablets, or seresto collars consistently year-round.
When should I take my dog to an emergency vet? +
Seek emergency care immediately for severe trauma, suspected poisoning, difficulty breathing, continuous vomiting, inability to urinate, or heatstroke.
