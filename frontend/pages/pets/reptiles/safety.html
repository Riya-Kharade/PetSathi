Essential Reptile Safety Tips

Safe Handling

Learn how your species tolerates handling. Move slowly, support the body, and let the reptile retreat when it needs a break.

Hand Hygiene

Wash with soap and running water before and after handling your reptile or its supplies.

Enclosure Safety

Close latches after every use and check that doors, lids, and cable openings are secure.

Temperature Control

Use thermostat-controlled heating and check the warm and cool areas against the requirements for your species.

Lighting Safety

Use species-appropriate lighting. If your reptile needs UVB, follow the bulb maker’s placement and replacement guidance.

Safe Decorations

Choose smooth, sturdy décor. Secure heavy pieces so they cannot shift or fall.

Clean Water

Offer fresh water in a species-appropriate setup and clean bowls or water features regularly.

Supervision

Keep other pets away during handling. Children under five should avoid contact with reptiles and their habitats.

Common Household Hazards

Toxic Substances

Store cleaners, sprays, and other chemicals away from the enclosure. Keep animals away while using them.

Unsafe Plants

Check that any plant in reach is safe for your species. Remove unidentified or treated plants.

Electrical Cables

Route cords outside the enclosure and keep water away from plugs and power strips.

Extreme Temperatures

Keep the enclosure away from direct sun, radiators, and drafts. Recheck temperatures during seasonal changes.

Small Objects

Remove loose pieces that could be swallowed or trap a foot or tail.

Other Pets

Use a closed room or secure barrier during handling. Keep dogs and cats away from the reptile and its enclosure.

Safe Reptile Handling

  • Approach calmly where the reptile can see you. Avoid sudden movements or grabbing from above.
  • Support its body with both hands when appropriate for the species. Never lift or pull it by the tail.
  • Keep sessions brief. Let the animal settle after a move or feeding, and stop if it tries to escape or shows distress.
  • If you are unsure how to handle your species safely, ask a veterinarian experienced with reptiles.

Enclosure and Habitat Safety

Secure Doors and Lids

Check latches, hinges, and lid clips regularly. Repair gaps before returning your reptile to the enclosure.

Proper Ventilation

Keep vents clear and choose ventilation suited to the species and humidity needs.

Guarded Heat Sources

Mount heaters securely, use a thermostat, and prevent contact with hot bulbs or surfaces.

Suitable Substrate

Choose bedding for the species, age, and health of your reptile. Ask your reptile vet if you are unsure.

Stable Décor

Check that branches, rocks, and hideouts cannot tip, collapse, or snag the animal.

Regular Cleaning

Remove waste and soiled bedding promptly. Clean equipment away from kitchens and food preparation.

Temperature and Humidity

Check the habitat with appropriate meters. Compare the readings with guidance for your species.

Hygiene and Disease Prevention

  • Wash your hands with soap and running water after handling the reptile, cleaning its habitat, or touching food, water, and equipment.
  • Clean reptile equipment outdoors when possible. Keep it out of food preparation areas and use dedicated tubs and tools.
  • Follow the label for any disinfectant approved for the equipment. Keep the reptile away during cleaning and follow rinsing and drying instructions.
  • Clean food and water containers regularly. Keep reptile supplies separate from household dishes and utensils.
