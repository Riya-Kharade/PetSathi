02 • LEARN TOGETHER

Training Through Trust

No force. No punishment. Just patience, clear signals and positive reinforcement.

⭐ Reward-based learning

Make learning a happy experience.

Reward your bird immediately when it voluntarily performs a desired action. Suitable food treats, praise or a favourite activity can be positive rewards.

Keep training gentle and short. Let your bird choose whether to participate.

1

Build familiarity

Start calmly. Let your bird approach at its own pace.

2

Reward progress

Mark and reward small voluntary steps towards the goal.

3

Practice gently

Repeat in short sessions and stop when your bird needs a break.

05 • UNDERSTAND, DON'T PUNISH

When Behaviour Changes

Explore common challenges. Click a card to see what may contribute and how to respond.

📢 Excessive Screaming

Frequent or sudden loud calling may relate to social needs, changes in routine, fear or other triggers.

Try: Observe patterns, check the bird's needs and provide appropriate enrichment. Avoid shouting back or punishment.
🪶 Feather Plucking

Feather damage can have medical, environmental or behavioural causes. It should never be assumed to be simply boredom.

Try: Arrange an avian veterinary assessment and review the bird's environment with professional guidance.
🦜 Biting

Biting may be a response to fear, discomfort, unwanted handling or a need for personal space.

Try: Notice warning signs, respect boundaries and reward calm voluntary interactions. Never hit or frighten your bird.
😕 Boredom & Stress

Limited enrichment, insufficient rest, unpredictable surroundings or unmet social needs may contribute to stress.

Try: Offer suitable foraging, enrichment, predictable routines and opportunities for quiet rest.
🩺
When in doubt, consult an avian veterinarian.

Sudden behavioural changes, feather damage, persistent screaming, unusual inactivity or aggression may need professional assessment. This guide is educational, not a diagnosis.
