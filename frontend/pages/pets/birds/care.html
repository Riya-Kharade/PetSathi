01 • EVERYDAY ROUTINE

Daily Bird Care

Small, consistent routines make a big difference. Observe your bird every day, keep its living space clean, and make time for gentle interaction.

💧

Fresh, Clean Water

Provide fresh drinking water every day. Wash the water bowl regularly and refill it whenever it becomes dirty or empty.

🌾

Suitable Food

Offer a balanced, species-appropriate diet. Ask an avian veterinarian about the right combination of formulated food, vegetables, and other suitable foods for your bird.

🧹

Clean Cage and Bowls

Remove leftover fresh food, clean food and water dishes, and replace soiled cage liners. A clean cage also makes it easier to notice changes in droppings.

🫶

Daily Interaction

Spend calm, positive time with your bird. Talking, gentle training, supervised play, and foraging activities can support social and mental well-being.

02 • A PLACE TO THRIVE

Housing & Environment

Your bird's home should provide room to move, opportunities to explore, and protection from everyday household dangers.

🏡

Choose the Right Cage

Choose the largest practical cage that suits your bird's species and size. It should allow comfortable movement, climbing, and wing stretching. Bar spacing must be small enough to prevent your bird from getting its head stuck.

🌿

Perches for Healthy Feet

Provide secure, bird-safe perches with different diameters and textures. Arrange them so your bird can move comfortably without constantly sitting in the same position.

🧩

Toys and Enrichment

Offer suitable toys, climbing opportunities, and supervised foraging activities. Check toys regularly for loose parts, sharp edges, or damage that could cause injury.

🌤️

Comfortable Placement

Keep the cage in a calm, well-ventilated area away from draughts, cooking fumes, excessive noise, and direct heat. Avoid placing it beside radiators or hot appliances.

🕊️

Safe Movement and Exercise

Give your bird appropriate opportunities for movement and exercise. Any out-of-cage activity should be supervised in a bird-proofed room with windows and doors secured.

🧼

Clean Surroundings

Keep the cage, food area, and surrounding space clean. Use products known to be safe for birds, follow label directions, rinse surfaces thoroughly, and keep your bird away during cleaning.

🪺
A HAPPY HABITAT

More than just a cage.

Birds are active and intelligent animals. A thoughtful environment should include room to move, suitable perches, enrichment, social interaction, and a predictable routine. Adapt the setup to your bird's individual species and needs.

03 • NOTICE THE LITTLE CHANGES

Health & Wellness

Birds may hide signs of illness. Changes in normal behaviour, eating, activity, or appearance deserve attention.

🪶
Unusually fluffed-up feathers or a change in appearance.
😴
Sleeping more than usual or appearing unusually quiet.
🍽️
Eating or drinking noticeably more or less than usual.
💩
Changes in the colour, amount, or consistency of droppings.
🪽
Sitting at the bottom of the cage, weakness, or poor balance.
🔇
Reduced singing, talking, interaction, or normal activity.

🩺 Preventive Veterinary Checkups

Schedule routine wellness visits with an avian veterinarian. They can assess your bird's weight, diet, feathers, beak, nails, and general condition, and recommend a suitable follow-up schedule.

⚠️ Don't ignore these signs

Contact an avian veterinarian promptly if you notice a significant change in your bird's health or behaviour.

  • Wheezing, open-mouth breathing, or tail bobbing while breathing.
  • Loss of balance, severe weakness, or collapse.
  • Repeated vomiting or major changes in droppings.
  • Sudden loss of appetite or marked lethargy.
  • Bleeding, injury, or suspected poisoning.
✂️
Feather and beak care

Monitor feather condition, skin, beak shape, and nail length. Do not trim feathers, beaks, or nails yourself without proper training and veterinary guidance. Unusual overgrowth, breakage, or damaged feathers should be assessed by a vet.

04 • A BIRD-SAFE HOME

Safety Tips

Some common household products and everyday activities can expose birds to serious risks. Prevention is essential.

🚫 Household hazards to avoid

Non-stick cookware fumes

Overheated non-stick cookware and appliances with PTFE-type coatings can release fumes that are dangerous to birds. Keep birds away from kitchens and cooking fumes.

Smoke, sprays, and strong fragrances

Keep birds away from cigarette smoke, aerosol sprays, perfumes, paint fumes, and other airborne irritants.

Household chemicals and pesticides

Store cleaning products, insecticides, rodent poison, and medicines securely. Never use chemicals near a bird unless a veterinarian confirms they are safe.

Unsafe foods

Avoid offering chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, avocado, onion, garlic, or spoiled and mouldy food. Check unfamiliar foods with an avian veterinarian first.

Open windows and ceiling fans

Secure doors and windows before allowing supervised movement outside the cage. Switch off ceiling fans and cover large mirrors or windows where needed.

Electrical wires and hot surfaces

Keep birds away from exposed wires, open flames, hot cookware, and kitchen appliances. Never leave a bird unattended near cooking areas.

🤲

Gentle Handling

Allow your bird to become comfortable with you gradually. Use calm movements and positive reinforcement. Never squeeze its body or restrict its chest while handling.

🚪

Supervise Out-of-Cage Time

Keep windows and doors closed, remove nearby hazards, and supervise your bird throughout its time outside the cage. Keep other pets at a safe distance.

🧯

Plan for Emergencies

Keep the contact details of an avian veterinarian and an emergency clinic accessible. Know how to transport your bird safely if urgent care is needed.

05 • KNOW WHEN TO GET HELP

When to Visit a Vet

Birds can become unwell without showing obvious signs at first. Early veterinary attention can make an important difference.

📅

Routine Wellness Visit

Arrange regular checkups even when your bird appears healthy. Your avian veterinarian can advise how often visits are appropriate for its species, age, and health history.

🔎

Noticeable Changes

Contact your vet when your bird eats less, becomes unusually quiet, changes its droppings, loses balance, or develops abnormal feather or beak changes.

🚨

Emergency Attention

Breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, significant bleeding, serious injury, or suspected poisoning require immediate veterinary attention. Do not wait to see whether the condition improves.

📞
Keep an avian vet's contact details ready

Not every veterinary clinic has experience treating birds. Identify an avian or bird-experienced veterinarian before an emergency happens.

Important: This page provides general educational information and is not a diagnosis or a replacement for veterinary advice. Bird care requirements vary by species and individual health. Consult a qualified avian veterinarian for diet, housing, grooming, treatment, and emergency decisions.
QUICK ANSWERS

Bird Care FAQs

Helpful answers to common questions from bird parents.

How often should I clean my bird's cage?

Remove soiled liners and leftover fresh food regularly. Clean food and water dishes daily, and schedule a thorough cage cleaning based on your bird's needs and the level of soiling.

Can I let my bird fly freely inside my home?

Out-of-cage exercise can be beneficial, but only in a bird-proofed and supervised room. Secure windows and doors, switch off ceiling fans, and remove household hazards first.

Do birds need regular veterinary checkups?

Yes. Routine wellness visits with an avian veterinarian help assess your bird's general health and identify potential problems early. Ask your vet about an appropriate schedule.

How can I tell if my bird is unwell?

Changes in appetite, droppings, posture, activity, breathing, or behaviour can indicate a health problem. Contact an avian veterinarian promptly rather than waiting for symptoms to worsen.

Can I use regular household sprays around birds?

Avoid exposing birds to aerosol sprays, smoke, strong fragrances, and cleaning fumes. Birds have sensitive respiratory systems. Follow veterinary advice when choosing cleaning products.
