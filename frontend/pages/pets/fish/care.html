Start with the Habitat

Aquarium Setup

A thoughtfully planned aquarium gives fish room to swim, places to shelter and a stable environment that is easier to maintain.

Build a safe, suitable home

Choose an aquarium based on the adult size, number and needs of the species you plan to keep. A larger volume of water is generally more stable than a very small tank, but stocking levels and swimming space must still suit the fish.

Place the aquarium on a level, sturdy stand that can support its filled weight. Keep it away from direct sunlight, heaters, loud vibrations and busy areas where repeated disturbances may stress fish.

  • Rinse aquarium-safe substrate before adding it; avoid soaps and household cleaners.
  • Use aquarium-safe rocks, driftwood and decorations with no sharp edges.
  • Add suitable live or artificial plants and hiding spaces without overcrowding swimming areas.
  • Set up and cycle the aquarium before adding fish; do not treat clear-looking water as proof that it is ready.
The Foundation of Fish Health

Water Quality

Fish live in the water around them, so regular testing and careful water changes are essential even when the aquarium looks clean.

Test, prepare and refresh the water

Use a reliable aquarium test kit to monitor ammonia, nitrite, nitrate and pH. In a healthy, established freshwater aquarium, ammonia and nitrite should read zero. Nitrate is managed through appropriate water changes, plant growth and sensible stocking; acceptable levels vary with the species and setup.

When changing water, use a dechlorinator suitable for the tap water and match the replacement water's temperature as closely as practical. Make partial, regular water changes rather than replacing all the aquarium water at once.

  • Ammonia and nitrite are toxic to fish and should not be detectable in a stable, cycled tank.
  • Nitrate is a product of the aquarium nitrogen cycle and can build up over time.
  • Test more often when establishing a tank, adding fish or investigating unusual behaviour.
  • Never add untreated tap water directly to an aquarium.
Keep the Aquarium Balanced

Filtration and Cleaning

Filtration supports the aquarium's biological balance, while gentle routine cleaning removes debris without disrupting the beneficial bacteria.

Clean without disturbing the ecosystem

Select a filter suited to the aquarium's volume and inhabitants. Filters provide mechanical filtration to trap particles and biological filtration that supports beneficial bacteria. Some setups also use chemical media for specific purposes.

Maintain the filter according to its manufacturer's instructions. If filter media needs rinsing, use removed aquarium water rather than untreated tap water, and avoid replacing all biological media at once.

  • Check that the filter is running and water is flowing properly every day.
  • Use an aquarium-safe algae pad to clean the inside glass; avoid household detergents.
  • Siphon visible waste and debris from the substrate during water changes.
  • Keep the filter intake clear and clean equipment gently to preserve beneficial bacteria.
Keep Conditions Consistent

Temperature and Lighting

Different fish species have different temperature and lighting needs. Research the requirements of every species in the aquarium and keep conditions consistent.

Prevent sudden environmental changes

Use an appropriately sized aquarium heater when required and place a reliable thermometer where it can be checked easily. Set the temperature for the needs of the fish rather than relying on a one-size-fits-all setting.

Provide a consistent lighting schedule with a regular dark period so fish and plants have a predictable day-and-night cycle. A timer can help keep the schedule steady. Avoid placing the aquarium in direct sunlight, which can encourage excess algae and cause temperature swings.

  • Check the thermometer daily and confirm the heater is operating normally.
  • Match the temperature range to the species you keep.
  • Use a consistent lighting period and avoid leaving aquarium lights on continuously.
  • Make temperature adjustments gradually; sudden changes can stress fish.
Consistent Attention

Daily and Weekly Fish Care

Short, regular checks help you notice changes early and keep feeding, equipment and maintenance routines on track.

Make observation part of your routine

Observe fish during feeding and at other times of day. Look for normal swimming, breathing, appetite and interaction. Clamped fins, unusual hiding, gasping, loss of appetite or erratic swimming can signal stress or a water-quality problem and should not be ignored.

Feed an appropriate, varied diet in amounts the fish can consume promptly. Remove uneaten food so it does not decompose and affect water quality. Follow species-specific feeding needs rather than overfeeding.

  • Daily: observe behaviour, check temperature, confirm filter and heater operation, and feed appropriately.
  • Daily: check for uneaten food, dead plant matter and visible waste.
  • Weekly: test water as appropriate for the tank's stability and recent changes.
  • Weekly: perform a suitable partial water change and clean accessible surfaces as needed.
A Gentle Introduction

Fish Acclimation

Introducing new fish gradually helps reduce the stress caused by differences in water temperature and chemistry.

Introduce new fish safely

Before purchasing fish, check that the aquarium is cycled, appropriately sized and compatible with the species. Avoid adding too many fish at once. If possible, quarantine new arrivals in a separate, properly equipped tank to observe their health before introducing them to an established community.

To acclimate, follow guidance suited to the species and the seller's transport method. A common starting step is to float the closed transport bag in the aquarium briefly to equalise temperature. Then gradually mix small amounts of aquarium water into the bag or use a suitable drip-acclimation method when appropriate. Avoid prolonged exposure to transport water, and do not pour shop or transport water into the aquarium.

  • Dim the aquarium lights and minimise noise and disturbance.
  • Match temperature gradually and account for species-specific water needs.
  • Transfer fish gently with a net or suitable container; leave transport water out of the tank.
  • Monitor new arrivals closely for stress, illness and compatibility issues.
Quick Reminders

Six Essential Fish Care Tips

Keep these practical reminders in mind as you build a healthy, stable aquarium routine.

Aquarium Setup Tips

Choose a tank suited to adult fish size, provide open swimming space and hiding places, and cycle the aquarium before stocking it.

Water Quality Tips

Test ammonia, nitrite and nitrate, prepare replacement water with a suitable conditioner, and make regular partial water changes.

Cleaning Tips

Remove waste and uneaten food, clean aquarium glass with safe tools, and maintain filter media without stripping away beneficial bacteria.

Temperature Tips

Use a thermometer, set the heater for your species and maintain a regular lighting schedule while avoiding sudden changes.

Daily Care Tips

Observe fish behaviour, check equipment, feed suitable portions and keep a simple schedule for water testing and tank maintenance.

Acclimation Tips

Prepare the aquarium first, equalise temperature gradually, avoid adding transport water and monitor new fish after introduction.
