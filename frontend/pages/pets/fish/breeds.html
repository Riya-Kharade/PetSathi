Key Selection Factors

Choosing the Right Fish

Before introducing new aquatic companions, evaluate these five critical factors to ensure lifelong health, balanced tank chemistry, and peaceful cohabitation.

Aquarium Size

Always base tank size on adult dimensions, not juvenile store size. Larger water volumes dilute waste and provide biological stability that prevents toxic ammonia spikes.

Water Requirements

Every fish requires specific water parameters—pH, general hardness, and temperature. Never mix species with opposing chemical or thermal habitat preferences.

Fish Compatibility

Combine fish that share compatible temperaments and occupy distinct water column tiers (surface, mid-level, bottom) to prevent territorial aggression and fin nipping.

Maintenance Requirements

Assess the time commitment required for weekly partial water changes, filter maintenance, water testing, and gravel siphoning before purchasing high-bioload species.

Experience Level

Match fish choices to your aquarium experience. Beginners thrive with hardy livebearers or tetras, while sensitive discus or delicate saltwater reef fish demand advanced skills.

Diet & Feeding Habits

Ensure you can reliably supply species-specific diets, whether herbivorous algae wafers, micro-pellets, or specialized frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp.
