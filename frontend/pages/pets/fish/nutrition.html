Staple & Supplemental Foods

Types of Fish Food

Combining a high-quality staple diet with targeted supplemental foods ensures complete amino acids, vitamins, and natural foraging enrichment.

Flakes

Common everyday food for surface- and mid-water-dwelling aquarium fish such as tetras, guppies, and danios. Easy to crumble for smaller mouths.

Pellets

Nutrient-rich, low-waste food available in floating, slow-sinking, and bottom-wafer sizes to match different fish sizes and swimming zones.

Freeze-Dried Food

Convenient, shelf-stable treats such as bloodworms, tubifex, and brine shrimp. Presoaking briefly in tank water helps prevent air swallowing.

Frozen Food

Suitable supplementary protein options like frozen mysis shrimp, daphnia, and bloodworms that retain natural moisture and nutrients without live-parasite risk.

Live Food

Natural, movement-rich food options such as daphnia and newly hatched brine shrimp that stimulate natural hunting instincts and condition breeding fish.

Vegetable-Based Food

Spirulina algae wafers, kelp flakes, and blanched zucchini or spinach designed for herbivorous grazers, plecos, and plant-loving community fish.

Dietary Profiles

Feeding Guide by Fish Type

Every fish species has a digestive tract adapted to its natural diet. Match food composition to your fish's biological feeding group.

Herbivorous Fish

Examples: Mollies, Otocinclus, Bristlenose Plecos, and Tangs. Feed spirulina wafers, algae sheets, and blanched greens in small, frequent grazing portions.

Carnivorous Fish

Examples: Bettas, Oscars, Pufferfish, and Cichlids. Offer high-protein pellets, frozen bloodworms, krill, or brine shrimp in controlled portions to avoid digestive strain.

Omnivorous Fish

Examples: Goldfish, Guppies, Tetras, Angelfish, and Corydoras. Provide a balanced rotation of staple flakes or pellets paired with veggies and protein treats.

Routine & Portions

Daily Feeding Schedule

Consistent feeding intervals and the 2-minute portion rule keep fish energized while protecting your aquarium's biological filter.

Adult Community Fish

Feed once or twice daily, offering only as much food as your fish actively consume within 1 to 2 minutes. An occasional weekly fast day supports healthy digestion.

Juveniles & Fry

Growing young fish have high metabolisms and benefit from 3 to 4 tiny, finely crushed meals spaced evenly throughout the day.

Bottom & Nocturnal Feeders

Drop sinking wafers or pellets for plecos, loaches, and corydoras shortly after tank lights dim so daytime swimmers do not intercept their meal.

Frequency & Quantity Depend on Species, Age & Tank Conditions

Warmer tropical water increases fish metabolism, while cooler setups slow digestion down. Always adjust meal frequency and portion sizes based on your specific fish species, age, activity level, and current water parameters.

Feeding Hazards

Foods & Practices to Avoid

Protect your fish from digestive blockages, nutritional deficiencies, and sudden water pollution by steering clear of these harmful practices.

Unsuitable Human Food

Never feed bread, crackers, dairy, processed meats, or seasoned scraps—they cannot be digested properly and rapidly foul aquarium water.

Excessive Feeding

Dumping too much food at once causes uneaten decay, lethal ammonia spikes, cloudy water, and swim bladder or obesity issues.

Spoiled or Contaminated Food

Discard damp, clumped, or expired fish food, and never feed wild-caught insects exposed to lawn pesticides or pollutants.

Incorrect Food Sizes

Oversized pellets cause choking or spitting, while overly powdery food drifts into gravel uneaten by larger fish.

Best Practices

Practical Fish Nutrition Tips

Six simple habits to keep your fish energetic, colorful, and healthy while keeping aquarium maintenance effortless.

Provide a Balanced Diet

Rotate high-quality staple flakes or pellets with frozen, live, or vegetable foods so your fish receive complete nutrients and variety.

Avoid Overfeeding

Stick to small pinches consumed within 1–2 minutes; hungry fish looking active at the glass does not mean they are underfed.

Remove Uneaten Food

Use a siphon or turkey baster to remove leftover pellets and decaying vegetable slices promptly before they release ammonia.

Choose Species-Appropriate Food

Match food type and sinking speed to each species' feeding zone—surface flakes for top swimmers and wafers for bottom dwellers.

Maintain Water Quality

Clean, oxygenated water supports proper digestion and nutrient absorption; pair good feeding habits with regular weekly water changes.

Store Food Properly

Keep dry fish food sealed tightly in a cool, dark cabinet away from tank humidity and heat to preserve delicate vitamins.
