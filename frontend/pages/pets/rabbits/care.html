Essential Routines

Daily Rabbit Care Essentials

Rabbits have delicate digestive tracts and specific behavioral needs. Consistent daily routines keep their gut moving, prevent illness, and build lifelong trust.

Fresh Drinking Water

Provide clean, fresh water daily in a heavy ceramic bowl or clean sipper bottle. Bowls support natural drinking posture and higher hydration.

Unlimited Fresh Hay

High-quality grass hay (Timothy or Orchard) must make up 80–85% of your rabbit's daily diet to grind teeth and prevent fatal GI stasis.

Suitable Daily Food

Offer a measured cup of washed leafy greens (romaine, coriander, basil) alongside a modest portion of plain, unmixed high-fibre pellets.

Clean Living Space

Spot-clean litter trays daily, remove damp bedding, and sweep stray droppings to maintain clean air and safeguard sensitive respiratory systems.

Daily Exercise

Allow at least 3–4 hours of free-roam exercise in a bunny-proofed room so your rabbit can stretch, hop, sprint, and perform happy binkies.

Social Interaction

Spend gentle floor-level time reading or speaking softly to your bunny. Let them approach at their own pace to foster calm companionship.

Safe Habitat

Housing & Indoor Environment

Rabbits need generous space to stand on hind legs without touching the roof, execute three consecutive hops, and rest securely away from household noise.

Create a Happy Bunny Home

A thriving bunny space blends safe enclosure boundaries with ample mental stimulation, soft footing, and secure hiding areas for natural relaxation.

Minimum 12+ sq ft indoor pen space
Solid flooring with washable cotton rugs
Two entry/exit wooden hideout boxes
Temperature kept steady between 15°C–21°C

Room to Hop & Stretch

Avoid cramped pet-store cages. Use an exercise pen or whole puppy-pen setup so your bunny can stand tall on hind legs and stretch completely.

Resting & Hiding Shelters

As natural prey animals, rabbits require wooden tunnels and cardboard hideouts with two openings so they never feel trapped.

Flooring & Soft Bedding

Rabbits lack protective foot pads. Wire cage floors cause painful sore hocks (pododermatitis). Cover slippery floors with non-slip foam mats and rugs.

Temperature & Airflow

Rabbits are prone to fatal heatstroke above 26°C (79°F). Keep living quarters cool, well-ventilated, and far away from direct hot windows or heaters.

Food & Water Placement

Mount a large hay rack directly over the litter tray. Place clean ceramic water bowls nearby to promote natural, ergonomic grazing.

Safe Exercise Areas

Bunny-proof free-roam rooms thoroughly: protect electrical cables with heavy split tubing and block narrow access behind couches and appliances.

Coat & Body Care

Grooming & Coat Care

Rabbits ingest loose hair while grooming. Because they cannot regurgitate hairballs, routine brushing is vital to prevent dangerous intestinal blockages.

Regular Brushing Routine

Short-haired breeds benefit from weekly brushing with a soft slicker or rubber grooming mitt. Always brush gently following the direction of fur growth.

Long-Haired Coat Care

Breeds like Lionheads and Angoras require daily gentle combing to prevent painful mats from tightening around their jaw, neck, and underbelly.

Shedding & Molting Cycles

Rabbits experience heavy seasonal molting 3–4 times per year. Increase brushing to daily sessions to remove loose tufts before they can be swallowed.

Nail Checks & Trimming

Trim your rabbit's nails every 4–6 weeks using a dedicated small-animal nail clipper. Be careful to avoid clipping the sensitive pink quick.

Ear & Eye Wellness Checks

Inspect ears weekly for crusting, redness, or mite debris, and ensure eyes remain clear and bright without persistent weeping or discharge.

Never Bathe in Water

Submerging a rabbit in water causes severe hypothermia, panic, and heart shock. Use dry cornstarch spot-cleans or ask your vet for safe cleaning guidance.

Sanitary Living

Hygiene & Enclosure Cleaning

Good hygiene protects against ammonia buildup, respiratory distress, and flystrike. Establish daily, weekly, and monthly sanitation routines.

Clean Habitat Protocol

Natural, non-toxic cleaning agents keep your rabbit's habitat sparkling clean without exposing delicate respiratory tracts to harsh chemical fumes.

Diluted white vinegar (1:1 with water) dissolves calcium
Daily bottom checks prevent fatal summer flystrike
Zero artificial aerosols, bleaches, or plug-in fragrances
Bowls and bottles sanitized daily in hot soapy water

Daily Bedding Spot-Clean

Remove wet paper bedding and soiled hay corners each morning to keep the air completely fresh and prevent moisture from contact with bunny paws.

Washing Dishes & Bowls

Rinse food crocks and thoroughly scrub water bowls with warm water and pet-safe soap every day to stop slippery bacterial biofilm from forming.

Litter Tray Refresh

Empty and thoroughly disinfect litter boxes 2–3 times a week using diluted vinegar to dissolve stubborn white urine scale without corrosive chemicals.

Weekly Enclosure Wash

Vacuum and wipe down pen bars, wash cotton floor mats in fragrance-free detergent, and rotate play tunnels for a fresh, welcoming environment.

Rear End & Bottom Check

Check your rabbit's rear daily for soft cecotropes, urine scald, or wetness. A clean, dry bottom is the single best protection against deadly flystrike.

Rabbit-Safe Cleaning

Avoid commercial bleach and scented sprays. Distilled white vinegar mixed with water is naturally antibacterial, neutralizes odors, and is 100% pet-safe.

Mental & Physical Health

Exercise & Enrichment

Rabbits are agile runners who require daily mental stimulation, foraging opportunities, and chew-safe outlets to prevent destructive boredom.

Daily Supervised Roaming

Provide at least 3–4 hours of active movement outside the pen each day. Free roaming encourages jumping, sprinting, and healthy bone density.

Safe Indoor Play Zones

Equip play areas with non-slip rugs and secure barriers. Hard tile and laminate cause slips and deter rabbits from running comfortably.

Tunnels & Exploration Mazes

Connect cardboard maze boxes, willow tunnels, and cloth burrows to mimic subterranean warrens and satisfy natural burrowing curiosity.

Chew-Safe Wooden Toys

Offer untreated apple wood sticks, willow balls, and woven seagrass mats. Safe chewing channels instinct and trims continuously growing teeth.

Digging & Foraging Boxes

Create a dedicated dig box using untreated cardboard strips, dried orchard grass, or child-safe play sand to prevent carpet destruction.

Cognitive Puzzle Games

Hide small pellet rewards inside stacking cups, toilet paper rolls, and snuffle mats to stimulate your rabbit's remarkable problem-solving mind.

House Training

Litter Training Habits

Rabbits naturally choose specific corners to eliminate. By understanding their biology, you can easily train them to use a litter tray 100% of the time.

Spacious Litter Box

Select a large cat litter box or low-entry storage bin. The tray must be large enough for the bunny to sit inside completely and turn around easily.

Rabbit-Safe Litter Substrate

Use recycled paper pellets or compressed wood pellets. Never use clay, clumping, or scented cat litter, which can cause fatal intestinal blockages if eaten.

Place Hay In or Over Box

Rabbits naturally eat hay while eliminating. Position fresh timothy hay directly inside one half of the litter box to guarantee immediate use.

Consistent Spot Routine

Watch where your bunny naturally relieves themselves and place the litter tray in that exact corner. Add extra boxes in roaming rooms if needed.

Positive Reinforcement

Praise and offer a small sprig of fresh herb (like parsley or dill) when your rabbit hops into the tray. Never punish or yell for accidents.

Patience & Neutering

Hormones cause territorial marking. Spaying or neutering at 4–6 months resolves 90% of spraying and establishes flawless lifelong litter habits.

Social Wellbeing

Companionship & Social Care

Rabbits are gregarious, highly social creatures whose wellbeing depends on bonding, gentle companionship, and understanding prey body language.

Inherent Social Needs

In nature, rabbits live in large bonded warrens. Prolonged loneliness causes deep depression, decreased appetite, and lethargy in domestic rabbits.

Safe Bonding Process

Bonding two rabbits must be done slowly in neutral territory. Allow them to see each other through adjoining pens before supervised floor dates.

Gentle Floor-Level Bond

Sit flat on the floor with fresh greens and let your rabbit sniff you. Avoid looming from above or scooping them up quickly like a bird of prey.

Respect Resting Hours

Rabbits are crepuscular: most active at dawn and dusk. Never force interaction during their deep mid-day naps inside quiet hideouts.

Preventing Stress Signals

Watch for warning signs of stress: foot thumping, flattened ears, or rapid breathing. Provide an immediate safe escape route and quiet surroundings.

Neutered Companion Pairs

A spayed female and neutered male provide the most balanced, affectionate pair. Grooming each other builds unmatched peace and security.
