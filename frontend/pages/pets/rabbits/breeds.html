Meet the Wonderful World of Rabbit Breeds

From tiny, alert Netherland Dwarfs to gentle, giant Flemish rabbits, every breed has its own look and grooming needs. Explore ten popular breeds and learn what responsible everyday care involves before welcoming a rabbit home.

10 Popular Breeds

Find the Bunny Behind the Ears

Each profile covers approximate adult size, appearance, coat type, temperament tendencies and breed-specific care. Individual rabbits can differ, so meet the rabbit rather than choosing by breed description alone.

🐰Holland Lop
01
Small • Lop-eared

Holland Lop

A compact rabbit with a broad head, short ears that hang down and a rounded body. Holland Lops are often described as curious and people-friendly, but each rabbit has an individual personality and preferred way of interacting.

Adult sizeAbout 0.9–1.8 kg
AppearanceCompact, drooping ears
Coat typeShort, dense, normal fur

Temperament: Often curious, playful and affectionate when handled gently.

Care: Brush weekly and more during shedding. Check ears and teeth regularly; provide unlimited grass hay, fresh water and daily room to move.

Photo: Harold Cecchetti · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

🐰Netherland Dwarf
02
Dwarf • Smallest type

Netherland Dwarf

One of the smallest domestic rabbit breeds, recognised by its very compact body, rounded head and short upright ears. These rabbits are often lively and alert; some may be more cautious around unfamiliar people and need patient, floor-level interaction.

Adult sizeAbout 0.5–1.1 kg
AppearanceRound head, upright ears
Coat typeShort, fine fur

Temperament: Alert, energetic and sometimes reserved until trust develops.

Care: A short coat needs light weekly brushing. Handle calmly, support the whole body and never assume a tiny rabbit needs less exercise space.

Photo: Nandhiniraji · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

🐰Lionhead
03
Small • Mane coat

Lionhead

The Lionhead is named for the woolly mane that frames its head, giving it a distinctive lion-like outline. It has a compact body and upright ears. Many are curious and playful, while comfort with handling varies from rabbit to rabbit.

Adult sizeAbout 1.1–1.7 kg
AppearanceHead-framing mane
Coat typeShort coat + wool mane

Temperament: Often active, inquisitive and sociable with patient care.

Care: Gently comb the mane several times a week and more often during moulting. Check for tangles around the face, eyes and hindquarters.

Photo: HutchRock · Wikimedia Commons · CC0

🐰Mini Rex
04
Small • Plush coat

Mini Rex

Mini Rex rabbits are known for a dense, springy coat that feels unusually soft and velvety. They have a compact, well-rounded body and upright ears. They are often curious and calm, and usually enjoy exploring a safe space at their own pace.

Adult sizeAbout 1.4–2.0 kg
AppearanceCompact, rounded body
Coat typeShort, plush Rex fur

Temperament: Commonly curious, gentle and easy to observe during play.

Care: Use gentle, occasional brushing rather than aggressive grooming. Provide safe flooring, enrichment and daily exercise; check skin and paws during routine handling.

Photo: Samantha Luckett · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

🐰Dutch Rabbit
05
Small to medium • Marked coat

Dutch Rabbit

The Dutch rabbit is recognised by its distinctive pattern: a white blaze on the face, white front, and a clear band separating the coloured rear section. Its neat, compact shape and classic markings make it easy to recognise.

Adult sizeAbout 1.6–2.5 kg
AppearanceWhite blaze and saddle
Coat typeShort, smooth fur

Temperament: Often sociable and adaptable; early gentle socialisation helps build trust.

Care: Brush weekly, keep nails trimmed and provide daily exercise. Like every rabbit, it needs hay, fresh water, a suitable diet and a spacious safe area.

Photo: Sahrazad · Wikimedia Commons · Public domain

🐇Flemish Giant
06
Giant • Large companion

Flemish Giant

One of the largest domestic rabbit breeds, the Flemish Giant has a long, powerful body, broad head and upright ears. The breed is often described as docile and steady, but its size makes suitable housing and confident, careful handling essential.

Adult size13 lb / 5.9 kg and above
AppearanceVery large, long body
Coat typeShort, dense fur

Temperament: Commonly gentle and calm when handled respectfully.

Care: Plan for a much larger exercise area, sturdy hideouts and an appropriately sized litter area. Support the chest and hindquarters when lifting; never pick up by the ears.

Photo: Oleg Yunakov · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

🐰English Angora
07
Medium • Wool coat

English Angora

The English Angora is covered in long, fine wool, including around its face and ears. Its soft, fluffy appearance is striking, but the coat is a serious daily-care commitment and can hide tangles or skin problems if neglected.

Adult sizeAbout 2.3–3.2 kg
AppearanceWoolly face and body
Coat typeLong, dense wool

Temperament: Often calm and gentle, though handling preferences vary.

Care: Requires thorough daily grooming and a planned clipping routine. Keep the face clear, check the skin and seek rabbit-experienced grooming or veterinary help when mats form.

Photo: Betty Chu · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0 / GFDL

🐰Himalayan Rabbit
08
Small • Colour-point

Himalayan Rabbit

Himalayans have a distinctive slender, cylindrical body and a mostly white coat with darker colour on the nose, ears, feet and tail. This colour-point pattern makes the breed recognisable; the coat is short and close lying.

Adult sizeAbout 1.1–2.0 kg
AppearanceWhite with dark points
Coat typeShort, fine fur

Temperament: Often described as gentle and patient; personality is individual.

Care: Light weekly brushing is usually enough outside moulting periods. Keep the living area cool, dry and well ventilated, and provide daily movement and hiding places.

Photo: SableSteel · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

🐰Harlequin Rabbit
09
Medium • Patterned coat

Harlequin Rabbit

Harlequins stand out for their contrasting colour pattern, often arranged in alternating bands or patches. The Japanese variety combines orange with a darker colour, while magpie varieties use white in place of orange.

Adult sizeAbout 2.7–4.1 kg
AppearanceDistinct two-colour pattern
Coat typeShort, rollback fur

Temperament: Often active and curious; regular enrichment supports natural exploration.

Care: Brush weekly and increase grooming during shedding. Offer safe toys, tunnels, digging opportunities and a secure area for daily exercise.

Photo: Meltingangels · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

🐰New Zealand Rabbit
10
Large • Powerful build

New Zealand Rabbit

New Zealand rabbits have a broad, muscular body and upright ears. White is a familiar variety, but the breed also appears in other recognised colours. They are often described as steady and calm, although no breed guarantees a particular personality.

Adult sizeAbout 4.1–5.0 kg
AppearanceBroad, muscular body
Coat typeShort, dense fur

Temperament: Commonly calm and steady when socialised gently.

Care: Brush weekly and provide ample floor space for running and stretching. Use a large, stable litter area and monitor weight with a rabbit-savvy vet.

Photo: Gb1 · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

A Happy Rabbit Needs More Than a Cute Face

Breed can help you anticipate size and grooming, but every domestic rabbit needs a safe, spacious home, a suitable high-fibre diet, daily activity and calm interaction. Rabbits are prey animals: many feel safer being approached at floor level than being picked up.

🥬 Food & waterProvide unlimited grass hay, fresh drinking water, suitable leafy greens and measured rabbit pellets.
🏡 Space & enrichmentUse a roomy, secure living area with space to stretch, run, hide, dig and chew safe materials.
🩺 Health & companyArrange rabbit-savvy veterinary care, routine checks and carefully planned companionship with a compatible rabbit.
Important: Never choose a rabbit only because of its breed or appearance. Meet the individual animal, ask about its health history and daily needs, and consider adoption from a responsible rescue. In warm climates, provide shade, ventilation and a reliably cool environment; rabbits can be vulnerable to overheating.
