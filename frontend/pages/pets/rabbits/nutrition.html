Essential Dietary Science

Foundations of Rabbit Nutrition

Rabbits are specialized hindgut fermenters. Their complex digestive anatomy depends on constant, high-fibre intake to stimulate peristalsis, wear down continuously growing teeth, and nourish beneficial cecal flora.

High-Fibre Gut Motility

Indigestible fibre keeps food moving smoothly through the digestive tract. Without constant fibre, a rabbit's digestive tract slows down rapidly, precipitating life-threatening gastrointestinal stasis.

Vital Gut Motility

Indispensable Hay Foundation

Feeding hay and pasture grass must represent at least 80% to 85% of total food volume daily. Constant chewing provides essential fiber and creates the side-to-side grinding action that wears open-rooted teeth.

85% Daily Volume

Fresh Hydrating Greens

Washed leafy greens deliver vital cellular hydration, key vitamins (A, C, K), and minerals. Rotating varieties stimulates natural foraging behaviors and provides mental enrichment for indoor companions.

10% Daily Greens
Golden Feeding Ratio

A Balanced Diet for Every Bunny

Veterinary consensus worldwide highlights this simple, life-saving nutritional proportion for healthy adult rabbits. Daily volume must be anchored firmly by endless grass hay, supported by fresh leafy vegetables and measured pellets.

85%
Grass Hay & Pasture Grass

Unlimited fresh Timothy, Orchard, or Meadow hay available 24/7 for digestive motility and dental wear.

10%
Fresh Leafy Vegetables

A packed adult-fist-sized portion of varied safe leafy greens washed clean daily for hydration and micronutrients.

5%
Measured Plain Pellets

1 to 2 tablespoons of uniform high-fibre pellets daily to supply concentrated vitamins and trace minerals.

24/7
Fresh Drinking Water

Continuous clean, pure drinking water served in heavy ceramic bowls and refreshed every single day.

The Foundation of Life

Wholesome Hay & Pasture Grass

Rabbit teeth grow continuously throughout life—up to 2 to 3 millimeters every week! Chewing high-silica grass hay naturally grinds incisors and molars down, preventing painful spurs while driving healthy digestion.

Gold Standard

Timothy Hay

The gold-standard daily grass hay for adult rabbits. High in coarse indigestible fibre with low protein and balanced calcium, making it ideal for lifelong gut health and weight management.

Daily Adult Staple
Soft & Sweet

Orchard Grass

A soft, fragrant, sweet-tasting grass hay that picky bunnies adore. Exceptional alternative for pet owners who experience pollen allergies from standard Timothy hay.

Picky Eater Friendly
Mixed Pasture

Meadow Hay

Rich botanical blend of varied pasture grasses, seed heads, and dried meadow herbs. Provides tactile variety, encouraging natural foraging exploration and prolonged chew time.

Foraging Variety
Dental Wear

Oat Hay

Features thick, crunchy golden stalks and immature seed heads. The coarse texture provides excellent abrasive dental grinding for rabbits prone to molar points or dental irregularities.

Dental Health
Fresh & Clean

Fresh Grass

Hand-picked, chemical-free garden grass adds succulent hydration and natural vitamins. Always introduce slowly; never feed lawnmower clippings, which ferment rapidly and cause fatal bloat.

Hand-Picked Only
Life Stage Care

Choosing by Age & Need

Growing junior rabbits under 6 months benefit from protein-rich Alfalfa (lucerne) hay. Adult rabbits (7+ months) must transition to Timothy, Orchard, or Meadow hay to prevent kidney calcium deposits and obesity.

Life Stage Care
Hydrating Garden Bounty

Fresh Vegetables & Leafy Greens

A vibrant daily salad supplies essential moisture and micronutrients. Introduce one new green at a time over 3 to 5 days, wash thoroughly, portion sensibly, and observe your rabbit's droppings closely.

Daily Green

Romaine Lettuce

Dark, crisp, hydrating, and packed with fibre. A dependable daily salad base. Never feed iceberg lettuce, which contains lactucarium that can trigger painful diarrhoea.

Dark Leafy Base
Gentle Digestion

Coriander / Cilantro

Wonderfully fragrant and universally adored by rabbits. Gentle on sensitive digestive systems and rich in vitamins A, C, and K to support healthy eyes and coat.

Aromatic Favorite
Nutrient Rich

Fresh Parsley

Both curly and flat-leaf parsley offer robust flavours and rich minerals. Moderate daily portions and rotate with other greens due to naturally higher oxalic acid and calcium content.

Vitamin-Packed
Calming Herb

Sweet Basil

Sweet, tender herb with soothing digestive properties. Great for encouraging appetite in hesitant bunnies and adding exciting olfactory enrichment to daily forage piles.

Digestive Soothing
High Moisture

Bok Choy & Greens

Crunchy, hydrating stems and succulent tender leaves. Gentle on rabbit stomachs while offering clean hydration and trace minerals. Excellent rotational green.

Crunchy Hydration
Foraging Fun

Mint, Dill & Carrot Tops

Carrot greens (the leafy fronds) make a wonderful regular leafy food! Combined with fresh peppermint, spearmint, and dill, they keep mealtimes joyful and gut-healthy.

Safe Herb Blend
Concentrated Micronutrients

Choosing & Measuring Rabbit Pellets

Pellets are designed as a small daily nutritional safety net, not an all-you-can-eat meal. Selecting high-fibre, uniform extruded pellets and strictly measuring portions prevents obesity and tooth misalignment.

Choose Plain Extruded Pellets

Always choose plain, uniform green extruded pellets. Target a nutritional profile of at least 20% to 25% crude fibre, 12% to 14% protein, and less than 2% fat for adult maintenance.

High-Fibre Extruded

Avoid Muesli & Seed Mixes

Colourful seed, corn, and nut mixes permit selective feeding: rabbits pick out the sugary grains and leave behind fibrous pieces. This causes dangerous calcium imbalance, dental disease, and obesity.

No Muesli Mixes

Measure Portions Strictly

For an adult companion rabbit, feed only 1 to 2 level tablespoons per 5 pounds of body weight per day. Never top off an empty bowl during the day—let your bunny satisfy hunger with fresh hay!

1–2 Tbsp Daily

Age-Appropriate Formulations

Growing kits under 6 months need nutrient-dense Alfalfa-based pellets to support bone and muscle growth. Adults over 7 months must switch to Timothy-based pellets to avoid excess calcium and urinary sludge.

Timothy for Adults

Airtight, Fresh Storage

Store pellets in an airtight, opaque container in a cool, dark, dry pantry. Exposure to light and humidity degrades vitamins A, C, and E. Purchase fresh bags and use within 60 to 90 days of opening.

Store Cool & Dry

Foraging Pellet Enrichment

Instead of placing pellets in an open bowl, scatter them across a clean foraging mat or hide them inside toilet paper rolls filled with hay. This sparks natural searching instincts and burns mental energy.

Scatter & Forage
Occasional Delights

Fruits & Healthy Occasional Treats

Rabbits have an enormous sweet tooth, but fruit sugars (fructose) can ferment dangerously in the cecum. Treats should be reserved as rare training rewards—never exceed 1 teaspoon per 2 lbs body weight, 2 to 3 times weekly.

Deseeded

Apple Slices

Crisp, sweet, and highly motivating. Always remove the core and all apple seeds, as seeds contain amygdalin, a compound that hydrolyzes into toxic cyanide in small herbivores.

Deseed Completely
Antioxidant

Strawberries

Nutrient-rich, delicious, and antioxidant-packed. The green leafy calyx top of the strawberry is completely safe, highly fibrous, and equally enjoyable for your bunny.

Include Green Tops
Single Berry

Blueberries

Loaded with anthocyanins and vitamins. Because blueberries come in tidy single-serving portions, 1 or 2 berries make the perfect portion-controlled reward during litter training or bonding.

1–2 Berries Max
High Sugar

Banana Coins

Rabbits find banana utterly irresistible. However, banana is extremely dense in starch and sugars. Limit strictly to a single thin coin slice (quarter-inch) once or twice weekly at most.

Tiny Coin Slice
Hydrating Treat

Pear & Melon

Succulent, sweet, and water-dense fruit treats. Remove all seeds and rind. A thumbnail-sized cube provides a refreshing summer reward without overloading the cecum with sugar.

Thumbnail Cube
Avoid Processed

Avoid Commercial Yoghurt Drops

Store-bought yoghurt treats, honey seed sticks, and coloured biscuits contain milk proteins, saturated fats, and simple sugars that rabbits cannot metabolize. Fresh natural herbs make superior rewards!

Say No to Yoghurt
Toxic & Unsafe Foods

Foods to Strictly Avoid Feeding

A rabbit's delicate cecal microbiome cannot digest dairy, refined starches, or toxic phytotoxins. Ingesting these dangerous foods can cause rapid fatal dysbiosis, acute kidney injury, or fatal poisoning.

Chocolate & Cocoa

Contains theobromine and caffeine. Even tiny amounts trigger severe tachycardia, cardiac arrhythmia, seizures, and fatal central nervous system toxicity in rabbits.

Severely Toxic

Avocado

The skin, pit, leaves, and green flesh contain persin, a dangerous fungicidal toxin. Causes respiratory distress, congestive heart failure, and sudden death in small herbivores.

Lethal Persin Toxin

Onion, Garlic & Alliums

All members of the allium family (onions, garlic, chives, leeks, shallots) cause oxidative destruction of red blood cells, precipitating severe hemolytic anaemia and organ failure.

Destroys Blood Cells

Caffeine & Coffee

Coffee grounds, tea bags, soda, and energy drinks are extreme stimulants for a rabbit's sensitive cardiac muscle, causing rapid hyperthermia, tremors, and cardiac arrest.

Cardiac Stimulant

Alcohol & Fermented Drinks

Rabbits lack hepatic enzymes required to metabolize alcohol. Ingestion causes immediate systemic acidosis, profound hypothermia, respiratory depression, and death.

Extreme Neurotoxin

Bread, Crackers & Biscuits

Cookies, crackers, bread, chips, and pasta contain complex carbohydrates that overwhelm the small intestine, causing explosive Clostridium bacteria overgrowth and fatal enterotoxemia.

Gut Stasis Trigger

Unknown Garden Weeds, Rhubarb & Nightshades

Rhubarb contains caustic oxalates; tomato vines and raw potato skins contain solanine glycoalkaloids; and many garden ornamentals (foxglove, ivy, buttercups, yew, lily of the valley) are deadly. Never feed wild plants unless positively identified as 100% rabbit-safe!

Poisonous Plant Hazard
Daily Feeding Routine

Daily Routine & Feeding Schedule

Rabbits thrive on predictable routines. A steady feeding schedule keeps digestion moving smoothly, prevents gorging, and allows owners to immediately notice any subtle loss of appetite.

Morning (8:00 AM)

Fresh Hay & Pellet Ration

Discard yesterday's flattened hay and fluff up a generous fresh pile of Timothy hay as large as your bunny's body. Wash and refill water bowls. Provide half of the measured daily plain pellet ration.

Midday (1:00 PM)

Hydration & Forage Check

Check water bowl to ensure it remains clean and uncontaminated by hay. Fluff up feeding racks, hide a fragrant sprig of mint or basil in foraging boxes, and check for regular round droppings.

Evening (7:00 PM)

Fresh Green Salad & Restock

Serve a washed, dripping-wet bowl of 3 to 4 varied leafy greens (Romaine, coriander, parsley). Serve the remaining half of measured pellets and pack bedtime hay racks for overnight munching.

Individual Veterinary Guidance Note: Exact feeding quantities, caloric requirements, and mineral needs depend on your rabbit's breed size, life stage, body condition score, and underlying health. Always consult a qualified rabbit-savvy veterinarian before implementing major dietary changes.

Hydration Essentials

Fresh Water & Proper Hydration

A 4-pound rabbit drinks approximately as much water in a day as a 20-pound dog! Adequate fluid intake prevents dry, impacted gut contents and flushes excess dietary calcium out of the bladder.

Constant 24/7 Access

Clean drinking water must be accessible without interruption. Rabbits experiencing even temporary water deprivation will immediately stop eating dry hay, precipitating acute GI stasis within hours.

Never Let Run Dry

Heavy Ceramic Water Bowls

Scientific studies prove rabbits drink significantly more volume from open water bowls than from sipper bottles. A heavy ceramic crock prevents tipping and provides a natural, comfortable drinking posture.

Bowls Preferred

Daily Cleaning & Hygiene

Wash bowls and bottles daily with hot water and pet-safe mild soap. Water containers develop invisible bacterial biofilm within 24 hours that can harbour harmful pathogens and cause sour tastes.

Wash Every Day
Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Nutrition Questions

Clear, vet-approved answers to the five most essential rabbit feeding questions.

Why is hay important for rabbits?

Hay is the single most vital component of a rabbit's diet, representing at least 80% to 85% of what they consume. Its long-strand, indigestible fibre is essential to stimulate continual peristalsis in the hindgut, preventing fatal gastrointestinal stasis. Furthermore, chewing tough grass hay provides the necessary side-to-side abrasive grinding action that wears down their open-rooted teeth, which grow up to 2 to 3 millimeters every week.

Which vegetables can rabbits eat?

Rabbits thrive on dark, leafy green vegetables such as Romaine lettuce, coriander (cilantro), parsley, basil, mint, dill, bok choy, rocket (arugula), watercress, and carrot greens (the feathery tops). Avoid iceberg lettuce, which contains the chemical lactucarium and is mostly watery with little fibre, as well as vegetables prone to causing gas, such as cabbage, broccoli, and cauliflower. Always wash greens thoroughly and introduce new varieties one by one over several days.

How much pellet food should rabbits receive?

For an adult companion rabbit, pellets should be strictly measured out at approximately 1 to 2 level tablespoons per 5 pounds of body weight each day. Pellets should always be plain, uniform green extruded cylinders with at least 20% to 25% fibre. Avoid free-feeding pellets or buying muesli mixes with colorful seeds and dried corn, which encourage selective eating, severe obesity, and tooth decay.

Can rabbits eat fruits?

Yes, but only as a rare, occasional treat in very tiny quantities. Suitable options include deseeded apple slices, strawberries (with green tops), blueberries, raspberries, and thin coin slices of banana. High fruit sugars (fructose) can upset the cecal balance of good bacteria, causing severe digestive upsets. Limit fruit rewards to no more than 1 level teaspoon per 2 pounds of body weight, 2 to 3 times per week at most.

Which foods should rabbits avoid?

Rabbits must never eat chocolate, avocado, onions, garlic, leeks, chives, coffee, tea, alcohol, or human processed foods like bread, crackers, cookies, chips, pasta, and commercial yoghurt drops. Additionally, avoid feeding potato skins, tomato leaves and vines, rhubarb, wild mushrooms, and lawnmower clippings (which ferment rapidly and cause fatal bloat). Always contact a rabbit-savvy vet immediately if your bunny accidentally ingests a toxic item.
