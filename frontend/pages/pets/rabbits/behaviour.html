Understand Your Rabbit Better.

Every binky, nose twitch and gentle nudge tells a story. Discover what your rabbit's behaviour means, understand their needs and create an environment where they can feel safe, curious and happy.

Getting to know your rabbit

Understanding Rabbit Behaviour

Rabbits communicate through their movements, posture and everyday actions. Learning to recognise these behaviours can help you understand how your rabbit feels and what they might be trying to tell you.

Binkying

A joyful jump with a twist in mid-air. Binkying is one of the clearest signs that a rabbit is excited and happy.

Flopping

When a rabbit suddenly rolls onto its side and stretches out, it often means they feel comfortable and relaxed.

Thumping

A strong thump from the hind legs can signal fear, alert other rabbits to danger or express frustration.

Nudging & Licking

Gentle nudges may be a request for attention, while licking is commonly associated with affection and social bonding.

Remember: Every rabbit has a unique personality. Always observe their overall body language and usual habits rather than interpreting one action alone.
The language of rabbits

What Is Your Rabbit Trying to Tell You?

Rabbits may not speak like humans, but their body language says a lot. From ear movements to subtle sounds, every signal can offer clues about how they are feeling.

01

Ear Positions

A rabbit's ears help them detect sounds and express their attention or mood.

  • Ears forward: Curious or listening.
  • Ears relaxed: Often comfortable.
  • Ears tightly flattened: May indicate fear or discomfort.
02

Eyes & Expressions

Their eyes and facial expressions can provide clues about their surroundings and comfort.

  • Partially closed eyes: Often relaxed.
  • Wide eyes: May indicate alertness or fear.
  • Head lowered: May be inviting grooming or petting.
03

Body Posture

The way a rabbit sits, stretches or moves can reveal how comfortable they feel.

  • Relaxed stretching: Feeling comfortable.
  • Crouched and tense: May be frightened.
  • Hiding: Could indicate stress or a need for security.
04

Sounds & Vocalisation

Rabbits are generally quiet animals, but some sounds can communicate strong feelings.

  • Gentle tooth clicking: Often contentment.
  • Grunting or growling: May signal displeasure.
  • Loud tooth grinding: Can indicate pain or distress.
Read the whole picture: One signal does not always tell the full story. Observe your rabbit's ears, eyes, posture and surroundings together to better understand how they feel.
A little nature, every day

Let Them Be Rabbits

Digging, running and exploring are more than just playful habits. These natural activities keep rabbits physically active, mentally stimulated and engaged with their surroundings.

Digging

Digging is a natural instinct that allows rabbits to create burrows, explore the ground and express their normal behaviour.

Try this: Provide a digging box filled with rabbit-safe soil, hay or shredded paper.

Hopping & Running

Rabbits need space to hop, sprint, jump and stretch. These movements help them exercise and express their playful nature.

Try this: Create a spacious, rabbit-proof exercise area with tunnels and safe places to explore.

Exploring & Foraging

Rabbits naturally search for food and investigate their surroundings. Foraging gives them an opportunity to stay curious and mentally active.

Try this: Hide small portions of their usual food in hay or safe cardboard enrichment toys.
Play, discover & explore

Make Every Day an Adventure

Play and enrichment help keep rabbits curious, active and mentally stimulated. Simple toys and everyday activities can turn their surroundings into an exciting place to explore.

Safe Toys

Give your rabbit toys that encourage chewing, tossing, hiding and exploring. Different textures can make playtime more interesting.

  • Untreated willow and seagrass toys.
  • Plain cardboard boxes and tubes.
  • Rabbit-safe chew toys and hay balls.
  • Wide cardboard tunnels for hiding.

Interactive Activities

Encourage your rabbit to think, move and investigate through simple games that make everyday playtime more engaging.

  • Hide small portions of their usual food.
  • Create simple cardboard obstacle courses.
  • Encourage them to follow a food trail.
  • Try supervised target-training games.

DIY Enrichment Ideas

You don't need expensive toys to keep your rabbit entertained. Everyday household materials can become fun enrichment activities.

  • Stuff cardboard tubes with hay.
  • Make a digging box with shredded paper.
  • Hide food inside a plain paper parcel.
  • Fill an egg carton with hay and daily pellets.
Play safely: Always supervise your rabbit when introducing new toys. Choose non-toxic materials without staples, tape, sharp edges or small swallowable parts. Remove damaged toys and anything your rabbit starts eating in large quantities.
Friends, trust & connection

Rabbits Are Better Together

Rabbits are naturally social animals. They form meaningful relationships with compatible companions and can also develop trusting relationships with the people who care for them.

Rabbit Companionship

Rabbits often benefit from living with a compatible rabbit companion. Bonded rabbits can groom, rest, play and explore together.

  • Consider adopting an already bonded pair.
  • Choose companions with compatible temperaments.
  • Neutering helps reduce hormone-driven aggression.
  • Provide enough space and resources for both.

Interaction with Owners

Building trust takes patience. Rabbits feel more comfortable when interactions happen gently and on their own terms.

  • Sit quietly at your rabbit's level.
  • Allow them to approach and investigate.
  • Offer gentle head strokes if welcomed.
  • Use positive reinforcement during play and training.

Safe Introductions

Introducing unfamiliar rabbits requires preparation and supervision. Never assume that two rabbits will immediately get along.

  • Get veterinary advice before bonding.
  • Ensure both rabbits are neutered and healed.
  • Start with separate nearby enclosures.
  • Use a neutral, rabbit-safe introduction area.
  • Supervise every face-to-face interaction.
Give bonding time: Never force rabbits to interact. Watch for positive behaviours such as mutual grooming and resting together. Persistent chasing, biting or serious fighting requires immediate intervention. Seek help from a rabbit-savvy veterinarian or qualified behaviourist if bonding becomes difficult.
Recognising when something is wrong

When Behaviour Tells You Something Is Wrong

Rabbits can hide signs of pain and illness. Paying attention to changes in their usual behaviour, eating habits and body language can help you recognise when they need support.

Signs of Stress or Fear

A frightened or uncomfortable rabbit may change its posture, become withdrawn or try to avoid interaction.

  • Hiding more than usual or avoiding people.
  • Crouching with a tense body and flattened ears.
  • Repeated thumping or trying to escape.
  • Excessive grooming or chewing enclosure bars.

Unusual Behaviour

Sudden changes from your rabbit's normal routine may indicate discomfort, pain or an underlying health problem.

  • Eating or drinking noticeably less.
  • Smaller, fewer or unusual droppings.
  • Unusual tiredness or reduced movement.
  • Sudden aggression or loss of interest in activities.

When to Seek Professional Help

Do not assume every behaviour change is simply a mood or personality issue. A rabbit-savvy veterinarian can help identify the cause.

  • Contact a vet if behaviour changes suddenly.
  • Seek advice for persistent stress or fear.
  • Ask a vet to assess signs of pain or illness.
  • Consult a qualified rabbit behaviourist for ongoing behavioural issues.
Important: Recognise emergencies. If your rabbit stops eating, eats significantly less or produces fewer or no droppings, contact a veterinarian immediately. These can be signs of a serious digestive problem and should never be ignored.

Never punish a frightened rabbit or force it to interact. Give it a safe place to retreat, observe changes in its normal routine and seek professional advice when needed.

The little things matter

Listen. Learn. Bond. 💚

Every rabbit has its own personality. Give them patience, opportunities to explore and a relationship built on trust.
