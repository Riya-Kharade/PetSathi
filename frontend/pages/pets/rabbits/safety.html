Rabbit-Proof Your Space

Creating a Safe Home

A rabbit-safe home starts at floor level. Walk through every room your rabbit can reach, look for anything that can be chewed, swallowed, tipped, or fallen from, and make the space secure before free-roaming begins.

Rabbit-Proof the Living Space

Check each room from your rabbit's point of view. Move cables, plants, small objects, and chewable materials out of reach.

Provide a Secure Enclosure

Choose a spacious pen or rabbit-proofed room with a hideout, litter tray, hay, water, and non-slip flooring.

Cover Exposed Wires

Use cable covers, trunking, or solid barriers. Unplug unused appliances and keep chargers off the floor.

Remove Dangerous Objects

Store sharp tools, plastic bags, string, rubber bands, coins, and medicines in closed drawers or high cupboards.

Offer Safe Hiding Spaces

Give your rabbit a quiet box or tunnel to retreat to. Check that it cannot become trapped or chew unsafe materials.

Close Off Unsafe Rooms

Use doors and baby gates to keep kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms, garages, and balconies out of bounds.

Know the Risks

Common Household Hazards

Many everyday items become dangerous when a curious rabbit chews, swallows, or becomes trapped. Check these hazards regularly, especially before free-roaming time.

Electrical Wires

Prevent chewing and contact with exposed wires. Cover, route, or block every cable your rabbit can reach.

Household Chemicals

Store detergents, bleach, disinfectants, and pest products safely in closed cabinets. Keep rabbits away while cleaning.

Small Objects

Keep choking hazards and dangerous items away. Plastic pieces, string, rubber bands, and coins are easy to swallow.

Furniture

Protect rabbits from chewing unsafe materials. Foam, upholstery, painted wood, and loose fabric can be harmful.

Stairs and Balconies

Prevent accidental falls with secure gates and barriers. Never allow unsupervised access to ledges or open balconies.

Other Pets

Supervise interactions with cats, dogs, and other animals. Always provide a secure retreat your rabbit can reach.

If your rabbit chews something dangerous

Remove access immediately and contact a rabbit-experienced veterinarian for advice. Do not wait for symptoms to appear, and do not give human medicines unless a vet instructs you to.

Indoor Safety

Electrical & Indoor Safety

Indoor free-roaming gives rabbits room to move, but it also gives them access to cables, heat sources, and furniture. Prepare the room before you open the pen.

Cover Cables and Wires

Use cable covers or trunking, route cords behind furniture, and unplug unused appliances.

Keep Away from Hot Surfaces

Block access to radiators, heaters, stovetops, irons, hot lamps, and fireplaces.

Secure Furniture

Anchor unstable shelves, lamps, and bins. Remove items that can tip, fall, or trap a curious rabbit.

Block Dangerous Gaps

Seal gaps behind appliances, under furniture, and around pipes where a rabbit could become stuck.

Remove Small and Sharp Objects

Keep plastic pieces, needles, pins, glass, and sharp tools out of reach. Offer safe chew toys instead.

Supervise Free-Roaming Time

Stay nearby while your rabbit explores. Close doors, watch for hazards, and guide your rabbit away from trouble.

Safe Food, Safe Plants

Food & Plant Safety

Rabbits have sensitive digestive systems. Keep unsafe human foods, toxic plants, and garden chemicals well out of reach, and check before offering anything new.

Unsafe Foods

Keep these foods away from your rabbit and out of reach in closed bins or high cupboards.

Chocolate

Contains theobromine and caffeine, which are toxic to rabbits. Keep all chocolate and cocoa products out of reach.

Avocado

The leaves, fruit, and pit contain persin, which can cause serious illness. Do not offer avocado in any form.

Onion and Garlic

Can irritate the digestive tract and damage red blood cells. Avoid onions, garlic, leeks, chives, and similar plants.

Caffeine

Coffee, tea, cola, and energy drinks can cause a dangerously fast heart rate, tremors, and distress.

Alcohol

Even small amounts can cause serious toxicity. Never leave alcoholic drinks where a rabbit can reach them.

Processed Foods

Sugary, salty, or fatty human foods can upset digestion. Keep biscuits, chips, cakes, and processed snacks away.

Plant Safety

Check every plant before your rabbit can access it, and keep garden chemicals securely stored.

Keep Unknown Plants Away

If you cannot confirm a plant is rabbit-safe, keep it out of reach. Many common houseplants and garden plants are toxic.

Check Plant Safety

Use a reliable rabbit-safe plant list or ask a rabbit-experienced veterinarian before offering any plant.

Prevent Pesticides

Keep fertilisers, weed killers, slug pellets, and treated plants away from rabbits. Never let them graze on sprayed grass.

Avoid Unknown Leaves and Flowers

Do not allow rabbits to chew leaves, stems, flowers, or bulbs unless you know exactly what they are.

When in doubt, keep it out

Plants and pesticides can be deceptive. A plant that is safe for people or other pets may still be dangerous to rabbits. If your rabbit eats an unknown plant or a treated leaf, remove access and contact a rabbit-experienced veterinarian for advice. Do not wait for symptoms.

Safe Outdoor Time

Outdoor Safety

Outdoor time can be enriching, but rabbits are prey animals and weather-sensitive. Use a secure run, check the area first, and keep your rabbit under close supervision.

Use a Secure, Supervised Enclosure

Choose a sturdy, predator-resistant run with a top and secure latches. Supervise all outdoor time.

Protect Rabbits from Predators

Foxes, birds of prey, cats, and dogs can attack quickly. Never assume a fence or hutch is enough on its own.

Provide Shade and Fresh Water

Offer a shaded shelter and constant access to fresh, clean water. Check the water regularly on warm days.

Avoid Extreme Heat and Weather

Skip outdoor time in very hot, cold, wet, or windy conditions. Rabbits can overheat or become chilled quickly.

Check for Harmful Plants

Inspect the area for toxic plants, bulbs, fallen leaves, and seeds before your rabbit goes outside.

Prevent Pesticides and Chemicals

Keep rabbits away from treated lawns, fertilisers, weed killers, and slug pellets. Ask before using any product.

Enrichment Outside

Safe Outdoor Adventures

Supervised outdoor time can offer fresh air, exercise, and enrichment. Use a secure run, provide shade and water, check the area for hazards, and never leave your rabbit unattended outdoors. Bring your rabbit inside if the weather changes or your rabbit seems stressed.

Gentle Hands

Safe Handling & Interaction

Rabbits are prey animals with delicate skeletons. Calm, low-stress handling builds trust and protects your rabbit from injury. Let your rabbit choose when to approach.

Support Chest and Hindquarters

If you must lift your rabbit, support the chest and hindquarters together and keep the body close to yours.

Never Lift by Ears or Legs

Lifting by the ears or legs can cause severe pain, injury, and lasting fear. Always use full body support.

Keep Handling Gentle and Calm

Use quiet movements and a soft voice. Let your rabbit settle before touching or picking it up.

Allow Voluntary Approach

Sit at floor level and let your rabbit come to you. Avoid chasing or cornering a frightened rabbit.

Supervise Interactions with Children

Show children how to sit quietly and stroke gently. Never let them carry or lift a rabbit unsupervised.

Avoid Sudden Movements and Noise

Loud sounds and quick movements can startle a rabbit. Create a calm space and approach slowly.

Rabbits have delicate bodies

Improper handling can cause serious injury, including spinal damage, broken bones, and internal injuries. If you are unsure how to lift or restrain your rabbit, ask a rabbit-experienced veterinarian or rescue to demonstrate safe handling.

Be Prepared

Emergency Preparedness

Rabbits can become seriously ill very quickly. Keep emergency details ready, know where to go, and act promptly if something seems wrong.

Save Emergency Contacts

Keep the phone number and address of a rabbit-experienced veterinarian or emergency clinic ready.

Know the Nearest Clinic

Find a clinic that treats rabbits before you need one. Confirm its opening hours and emergency arrangements.

Keep a Carrier Ready

Store a secure, well-ventilated carrier near your rabbit's supplies so you can leave quickly.

Prepare Veterinary Records

Keep vaccination, medication, and health records together in case you need to share them with a vet.

Know How to Respond

If your rabbit is injured or exposed to a dangerous substance, remove access, keep the rabbit calm, and call a vet.

Seek Immediate Veterinary Attention

Act quickly for serious injuries, breathing problems, suspected poisoning, or sudden illness.

Seek urgent help if your rabbit:

  • Stops eating or passing droppings, or eats much less than usual.
  • Has difficulty breathing, collapses, seizures, or loses balance.
  • May have eaten something toxic, or has been exposed to chemicals.
  • Is bleeding, badly injured, or has a suspected fracture.
  • Shows a head tilt, severe weakness, or a sudden change in behaviour.

Do not wait overnight or try home remedies. Call a rabbit-experienced veterinarian immediately.

Quick Answers

Rabbit Safety FAQs

Practical answers to common questions about keeping rabbits safe at home and outdoors.

How do I rabbit-proof my home?

Start at floor level. Cover or block electrical cables, remove small objects and toxic plants, protect furniture from chewing, and secure stairs and balconies. Provide a spacious enclosure with a hideout, litter tray, hay, water, and non-slip flooring, and supervise free-roaming time.

Which household plants are safe for rabbits?

Many common houseplants and garden plants are unsafe. Check a reliable rabbit-safe plant list or ask a rabbit-experienced veterinarian before offering any plant. Keep unknown plants out of reach, and never let your rabbit eat plants treated with pesticides or fertilisers.

How can I protect electrical wires from my rabbit?

Use cable covers or trunking, route cords behind furniture or above rabbit height, unplug unused appliances, and block access to areas where wires cannot be hidden. Supervise free-roaming time and check regularly for new cables or chewed covers.

How can my rabbit play safely outdoors?

Use a secure, predator-resistant run with a top and secure latches. Provide shade and fresh water, check for harmful plants and pesticides, avoid extreme weather, and never leave your rabbit unattended outdoors. Bring your rabbit inside if it seems stressed or the weather changes.

What is the safest way to handle a rabbit?

Sit at floor level and let your rabbit approach you. If you must lift your rabbit, support the chest and hindquarters together, keep the body close to yours, and never lift by the ears or legs. Keep handling gentle and calm, and ask a rabbit-experienced veterinarian or rescue for a demonstration if you are unsure.
