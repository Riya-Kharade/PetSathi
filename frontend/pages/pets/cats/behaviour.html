Understand Your Cat Better

Discover what your cat's behaviour means, learn how they communicate and explore simple ways to keep them happy, active and comfortable

Explore Cat Behaviour ↓

Purring

Cats often purr when they feel relaxed and comfortable. However, they may also purr when stressed or in pain.

Kneading

Kneading happens when cats rhythmically push their paws against soft surfaces. It is often associated with comfort and relaxation.

Tail Movements

A cat's tail can reveal its mood. An upright tail may indicate friendliness, while a rapidly flicking tail can signal irritation.

Scratching

Scratching helps cats maintain their claws, stretch their bodies, and mark their territory. Provide suitable scratching posts.

Hiding

Cats may hide when they feel nervous, overwhelmed, or simply want some quiet time. Give them a safe space to retreat to.

Rubbing

When cats rub against people or objects, they often leave their scent behind. This can be a way of marking familiar surroundings.

Meowing

Meowing, chirping, and other sounds are ways cats communicate. Their meaning can depend on the situation and the individual cat.

Cat Communication

Cats communicate through their body language, expressions and sounds. Learning these signals helps you understand how your cat feels and what they need.

Body Language

A cat's posture can reveal a lot about its mood. A relaxed body often suggests comfort, while a tense or crouched posture may indicate fear or uncertainty.

Ear & Tail Positions

Forward-facing ears often show interest, while flattened ears can signal fear or irritation. An upright tail commonly indicates a friendly or confident mood.

Eye Contact & Blinking

Slow blinking can be a sign of trust and comfort. Avoid forcing eye contact, especially when a cat appears nervous or uncomfortable.

Vocal Communication

Meowing, chirping, purring, hissing and growling all communicate different things. Their meaning depends on the situation and your cat's behaviour.

Signs of Comfort & Stress

Relaxed muscles, normal grooming and comfortable exploration can indicate that a cat feels safe. Hiding, flattened ears or a tense posture may signal stress.

Training & Socialisation

Cats learn best through patience, consistency and positive experiences. Small steps can help them feel confident in their surroundings.

Litter Training

Place a clean litter box in a quiet, accessible location. Keep it clean and help your cat become familiar with it. Reward consistent use and avoid punishment for accidents.

Positive Reinforcement

Encourage desirable behaviour with treats, praise or playtime. Reward your cat immediately after the behaviour so they can associate it with a positive experience.

Scratching Post Training

Place scratching posts near favourite resting areas or places your cat already scratches. Encourage their use with toys, treats and praise.

Introducing New Environments

Let your cat explore unfamiliar spaces gradually. Start with a quiet, comfortable room and allow them to explore at their own pace.

Meeting New People & Pets

Introduce new people and animals slowly. Give your cat a safe space to retreat to, supervise early interactions and never force contact.

Indoor Activities & Enrichment

Indoor cats need opportunities to explore, play and stay active. Simple activities can keep their minds stimulated and support their physical well-being.

01

Interactive Toys

Wand toys, moving toys and toy mice encourage natural hunting instincts while keeping cats physically active.

02

Puzzle Feeders

Make mealtimes more engaging with puzzle feeders that encourage cats to think, explore and work for their food.

03

Climbing Structures

Cat trees, shelves and climbing spaces provide exercise and elevated spots where cats can observe their surroundings.

04

Scratching Posts

Scratching posts help cats stretch, maintain their claws and express natural scratching behaviour.

05

Hide-and-Seek

Use cardboard boxes, tunnels and safe hiding spots to encourage curiosity and give cats places to explore.

06

Interactive Play Sessions

Set aside regular time to play with your cat. Short, engaging sessions help build confidence and strengthen your bond.

Building a Bond With Your Cat

Every cat has its own personality and way of showing affection. A strong bond grows through patience, understanding and everyday positive experiences.

Respect Their Personal Space

Allow your cat to approach you when they feel comfortable. Avoid forcing cuddles or handling and give them a quiet place to retreat whenever they need it.

Make Time for Play

Set aside time each day for interactive play. Follow your cat's energy levels and use toys that encourage their natural curiosity.

Create a Comfortable Environment

Provide cosy resting spots, clean litter areas, fresh water and safe spaces where your cat can relax and feel secure.

Understand Their Preferences

Some cats enjoy being petted, while others prefer sitting nearby. Notice what your cat enjoys and respect their individual personality.

Build Trust Through Positive Interaction

Use gentle handling, patience, treats and positive experiences to help your cat feel safe and develop confidence around you.
