Reptile Care & Terrarium Guide

Reptiles bring the mystery and fascination of ancient wildlife into our homes. Because they rely on their environment to regulate metabolism and digestion, keeping reptiles is a rewarding science of temperature gradients, specialized UVB lighting, humidity balance, and species-tailored nutrition.

Terrarium Companions

Popular Reptile Species

Discover the distinct behaviors, adult sizes, and environmental criteria for companion reptiles.

Diurnal • Omnivore

Bearded Dragon

Pogona vitticeps

Among the most popular and personable lizards in the world. Bearded dragons love human interaction and arm-waving communication. They require a 4x2x2 foot minimum enclosure with high-intensity desert UVB.

Lifespan10 – 15 Years
Basking Temp100°F – 105°F
Min Enclosure120 Gallon (4x2x2)
LightingT5 HO 10.0 / 12% UVB
Crepuscular • Insectivore

Leopard Gecko

Eublepharis macularius

Gentle, slow-moving, and easy to handle. Leopard geckos store energy reserves in their plump tails. They need a designated warm side, a cool side, and a moist humid hide box to shed their skin cleanly.

Lifespan15 – 20 Years
Warm Hide88°F – 92°F
Min Enclosure20 – 40 Gallon Long
DietGut-loaded Insects
Semi-Aquatic • Basking

Red-Eared Slider Turtle

Trachemys scripta elegans

Lively swimmers that require both deep swimming space and a completely dry haul-out basking platform. They are heavy bio-load producers, demanding canister filters rated for 2–3 times the tank water volume.

Lifespan25 – 40+ Years
Tank Guideline10 Gallon per Shell Inch
Water Temp75°F – 78°F
Basking Dock88°F – 92°F + UVB
Docile • Carnivore

Corn Snake

Pantherophis guttatus

Widely praised as the quintessential beginner snake due to their docile temperament and modest humidity requirements. Escape-proof enclosures with locking clips are mandatory as snakes are notorious escape artists.

Lifespan15 – 20 Years
Warm Side85°F (Thermostat Safe)
Min Enclosure40 – 75 Gallon
DietFrozen-Thawed Rodents
Nocturnal • Carnivore

Ball Python

Python regius

A stocky African python that curls into a ball when stressed. This long-lived species needs consistent husbandry and a quiet, secure retreat.

Lifespan20 – 30+ Years
Adult Size3 – 5 ft
HabitatWest & Central Africa
DietThawed Rodents
Diurnal • Omnivore

Blue-Tongued Skink

Tiliqua scincoides

A sturdy Australian lizard known for its broad blue tongue. Blue-tongued skinks need a secure enclosure, a warm-to-cool gradient, and a varied omnivorous diet.

Lifespan15 – 20 Years
Adult Size18 – 24 in
HabitatAustralia
DietVaried Omnivore
Arboreal • Tropical

Crested Gecko

Correlophus ciliatus

Hailing from New Caledonia, crested geckos have adhesive toe pads and eyelash-like crests. They thrive in tall vertical arboreal terrariums at typical room temperatures without intense heat lamps.

Lifespan15 – 20 Years
Ambient Temp70°F – 78°F
Humidity60% – 80% (Misting)
Primary DietComplete Fruit Powder
Terrestrial • Herbivore

Russian Tortoise

Testudo horsfieldii

Hardy, terrestrial herbivores with curious personalities. Russian tortoises need extensive floor space (wooden tortoise tables or outdoor enclosures) with deep substrate for digging, plus high-fiber leafy weeds.

Lifespan40 – 50+ Years
Table Space4x4 to 6x3 ft Minimum
Basking Temp95°F – 100°F
Diet100% Weeds & Grasses
Physics & Biology

Heating & Full-Spectrum UVB Lighting

Reptiles cannot produce body heat or absorb calcium without proper lighting fixtures.

🌡️

Thermal Gradients

An enclosure must never be the exact same temperature throughout:

  • Provide a basking hotspot at one end and a cool refuge at the opposite end.
  • Always attach heating elements (ceramic heaters, heat mats) to a digital proportional thermostat to prevent burns.
  • Measure surface temperatures using an infrared temp gun.
☀️

Linear T5 High-Output UVB

UVB light enables reptiles to synthesize Vitamin D3 for calcium absorption:

  • Coil/compact bulbs are insufficient; use linear fluorescent T5 tubes that span ½ to ⅔ the enclosure length.
  • Without UVB, reptiles develop painful, irreversible Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD).
  • Replace linear UVB bulbs every 6 to 12 months, as UV output degrades before the visible light burns out.
🌙

Natural Photoperiod & Darkness

Reptiles need an uninterrupted 12-hour light / 12-hour dark circadian rhythm:

  • Turn off all visible lights at night.
  • Avoid colored night bulbs (red or blue light): Reptiles see colored light clearly, which disrupts their sleep cycles.
  • For supplemental night heat, use non-light emitting Ceramic Heat Emitters (CHE) or Deep Heat Projectors (DHP).
Safe Enclosure Setup

Humidity Control & Substrate Safety

Balancing moisture levels ensures clean shedding and prevents respiratory distress.

💧

Humidity Benchmarks

Monitor relative humidity with digital probe hygrometers:

  • Arid species (Bearded Dragons): 30% – 40% humidity (high humidity causes fungal and lung infections).
  • Tropical geckos & turtles: 60% – 85% humidity maintained with daily misting and damp sphagnum moss.
  • Always provide a humid micro-climate hide during shedding cycles.
🧱

Safe Bedding vs Impaction

Substrate choice must prevent accidental ingestion and gastrointestinal blockage:

  • Safe options: Slate tile, textured ceramic tile, paper towels, non-adhesive shelf liners, or organic topsoil/playsand mix (70/30).
  • AVOID Calcium Sand: Promoted by commercial stores but clumps into concrete-like masses in reptile intestines.
  • Avoid cedar or pine bark chips which release toxic aromatic oils.
🪨

Enrichment & Climbing

Mental and physical exercise through 3D terrain:

  • Add sterilized driftwood, cork bark rounds, basking slate, and climbing ledges.
  • Secure heavy rocks firmly to the tank floor so burrowing animals cannot cause collapses.
  • Provide at least two secure hideouts: one warm hide and one cool hide.
Dietary Precision

Feeder Insects, Greens & Calcium Dusting

Live insects and fresh plant matter must be enriched with critical vitamins.

🦗

Gut-Loaded Live Insects

Insects are only as nutritious as what they eat prior to feeding:

  • Feed live Dubia roaches, black soldier fly larvae, crickets, and silkworms carrots, collard greens, and sweet potatoes 24 hours before feeding.
  • Avoid wild-caught bugs which carry pesticides and parasites.
  • Offer mealworms and waxworms sparingly due to high fat content.
🧂

Calcium & Multivitamin Dusting

Dust live feeders and salads regularly:

  • Pure Calcium Carbonate (without D3): Dust on most insect feedings for animals with strong UVB lighting.
  • Calcium with Vitamin D3: Dust 1–2 times weekly for crepuscular species or low-UVB setups.
  • Reptile Multivitamin powder: Supplement once every 1–2 weeks to prevent Vitamin A deficiency.
🥗

Fresh Greens & Weeds

For adult omnivores and herbivores (Bearded Dragons, Tortoises):

  • High-calcium staples: Collard greens, dandelion greens, mustard greens, turnip greens, and endive.
  • Avoid high-oxalate greens: Spinach, beet greens, and rhubarb bind to dietary calcium and inhibit absorption.
  • Avoid iceberg lettuce which provides zero nutrition.
Husbandry Essentials

Reptile Care Do's & Don'ts

Fundamental rules every responsible reptile caregiver must follow.

✔ Care Do's

  • DO use a digital thermostat on all heating devices to eliminate the risk of fatal overheating and thermal burns.
  • DO replace linear UVB tubes every 6–12 months even if the light appears illuminated, as UV spectrum fades.
  • DO gut-load and dust feeder insects with calcium carbonate powder to prevent Metabolic Bone Disease.
  • DO wash your hands with antibacterial soap before and after handling reptiles to maintain hygiene and prevent Salmonella transmission.
  • DO provide a dedicated moist shedding hide lined with damp sphagnum moss or paper towel.

✖ Care Don'ts

  • DON'T use hot rocks (electric heat rocks); they have notorious hotspot failures that cause catastrophic belly burns.
  • DON'T house multiple bearded dragons or geckos together; they are solitary and territorial, often resulting in severe bite injuries.
  • DON'T rely on analog stick-on dials; they can be inaccurate by up to 20°F and 30% humidity. Use digital probes.
  • DON'T leave live crickets in the enclosure overnight; hungry crickets will chew on sleeping reptiles' eyes and skin.
  • DON'T feed wild-caught insects; they carry agricultural toxins, pesticides, and internal parasites.
