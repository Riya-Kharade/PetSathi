General Pet Care & Husbandry Guide

Whether your companion hops, scurries, swims, or basks, core husbandry principles remain universal. Learn safe non-toxic cleaning protocols, early illness detection methods, home first-aid preparation, and how to establish trusted care with specialized avian and exotic veterinarians.

Consistency Matters

Daily Husbandry Pillars

A few proactive minutes every day saves your pet from preventable diseases and stress.

💧

Daily Fresh Water Refresh

Standing water quickly harbors bacterial biofilms and fungal spores:

  • Empty, rinse, and refill water bowls and water bottles every single day.
  • For aquatic setups, check filters for continuous unblocked flow.
  • Use chlorine-free conditioned water for all small pets and amphibians.
🧹

Spot-Cleaning Soiled Areas

Remove waste promptly before harmful ammonia fumes accumulate:

  • Scoop damp bedding corners, feces, and uneaten fresh greens daily.
  • In terrariums, spot-clean urates and insect debris.
  • In aquariums, remove uneaten fish food within 5 minutes of feeding.
👀

Behavioral Check & Temperament

Prey animals instinctively mask their pain and weakness:

  • Observe posture, swimming depth, mobility, and appetite daily.
  • Hiding constantly, reluctance to eat, or lethargy is an early symptom of illness.
  • Check breathing patterns for clicking, wheezing, or open-mouth gasping.
Safe Chemistry

Enclosure Sanitization & Chemical Hazards

Small animals have hypersensitive lungs and permeable skin that cannot tolerate standard household cleaners.

🧪 Dangerous Cleaning Agents

Never use standard household cleaning chemicals in or near small animal enclosures, tanks, or terrariums. Even trace residues or residual fumes can trigger fatal respiratory arrest, skin burns, or toxic shock.

  • Bleach & Chlorine: Highly caustic; emits chlorine gas that burns delicate respiratory cilia.
  • Ammonia Cleaners (Windex): Lethal to birds, reptiles, and pocket pets; replicates urine decomposition.
  • Aerosol sprays, scented candles, & plug-in air fresheners: Toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs).
  • Pine-Sol and phenol disinfectants: Severely toxic to the liver and kidneys of small mammals and reptiles.

🌿 Safe & Approved Sanitizers

Clean habitats effectively without endangering your companion by using veterinary-grade, pet-safe alternatives:

  • Diluted Distilled White Vinegar: 50/50 mix with water dissolves mineral deposits, urine scale, and odors naturally.
  • Veterinary Chlorhexidine (Nolvasan): Highly effective hospital-grade antiseptic safe for cage and terrarium wipe-downs.
  • F10SC Veterinary Disinfectant: Broad-spectrum bactericidal, virucidal, and fungicidal spray safe when dry.
  • Baking Soda Paste: Excellent for scrubbing tough dried stains on glass before thorough rinsing with warm water.
Early Warning Signs

Health Tracking & Red Flag Symptoms

Catching health issues early is the key to successful treatment in exotic companion animals.

⚖️

Weekly Gram Scale Weighing

Weight is the most sensitive metric of health:

  • Place a digital kitchen scale (measuring in grams) on a flat counter.
  • Weigh your pet at the same time each week and log it in a notebook or phone app.
  • A loss of 10% body weight warrants an immediate exotic veterinary visit.
🩺

Physical Inspection Check

Conduct a gentle weekly head-to-tail physical exam:

  • Eyes & Nose: Should be clear, bright, and free of crust, discharge, or swelling.
  • Teeth & Mouth: Check for overgrown incisors, drooling (slobbers), or mouth rot.
  • Skin & Coat: Inspect for balding patches, mites, retained sheds, or scale rot.
🚨

Immediate Emergency Red Flags

Seek urgent veterinary attention if you observe:

  • Complete refusal to eat or drink for over 12 to 24 hours.
  • Severe lethargy, inability to support body weight, or head tilt.
  • Open-mouth breathing, audible wheezing, or raspy breathing.
  • Bloated, distended abdomen or severe diarrhea / lack of fecal pellets.
Disaster Preparedness

Emergency First-Aid & Evacuation Kit

Keep a dedicated emergency kit assembled and accessible in case of power outages or evacuations.

📦 Evacuation Supplies

Be prepared to evacuate on 5 minutes notice with these essentials packed in a sealable storage tote:

  • Secure Travel Carrier: Hard-sided carrier with secure latch, lined with absorbent fleece or towels.
  • 7-Day Food & Water Supply: Bottled water, shelf-stable hay, pellets, and non-perishable food.
  • Thermal Heat Packs: Uniheat 40-hour shipping warmers (never place directly against pet skin; wrap in towel).
  • Styrofoam Cooler: Essential for transporting aquatic fish or reptiles in stable temperatures during outages.
  • Battery-Powered Aerator: Keeps aquarium water oxygenated during municipal electrical blackouts.

🩹 First-Aid Medical Kit

Basic non-prescription medical tools for initial stabilization before veterinary arrival:

  • Critical Care Recovery Formula: Oxbow Critical Care herbivore or carnivore powder with feeding syringes (1ml, 10ml).
  • Styptic Powder / Cornstarch: Rapidly stops bleeding from broken nails or claws.
  • Sterile Saline Wash: Flushes debris or dust from eyes without irritation.
  • Digital Thermometer & Temp Gun: Measures pet body temperature and environmental basking spots accurately.
  • Exotic Vet Contact Sheet: Physical paper card listing regular and 24/7 emergency clinic phone numbers and addresses.
Professional Care

Finding & Partnering With an Exotic Veterinarian

Traditional dog and cat veterinarians rarely have the diagnostic equipment or surgical training required for exotics.

🎓

Specialty Associations

Look for veterinarians accredited by official specialty boards:

  • AEMV: Association of Exotic Mammal Veterinarians (guinea pigs, hamsters, rabbits, rats).
  • ARAV: Association of Reptilian and Amphibian Veterinarians (lizards, tortoises, snakes).
  • AAV: Association of Avian Veterinarians (parrots, finches, cockatiels).
📋

Pre-Visit Preparation

Make your exotic veterinary appointment as productive as possible:

  • Take photos or video of the home enclosure, lighting setup, and thermometers.
  • Bring exact brand names of pellets, hay, vitamin powders, and substrate.
  • Collect a fresh fecal sample in a clean zip-top bag for microscopic parasite testing.
🛡️

Preventative Wellness Visits

Annual exams prevent emergencies before symptoms arise:

  • Schedule a baseline wellness exam within 2 to 3 weeks of bringing any new pet home.
  • Routine wellness checks evaluate teeth length, heart sounds, joints, and parasite loads.
  • Establishes an existing doctor-client-patient relationship (VCPR) for urgent prescription access.
Universal Guidelines

General Husbandry Do's & Don'ts

Guiding principles that protect all companion species under your roof.

✔ Husbandry Do's

  • DO track weekly weight in grams; weight decline is the single most reliable early warning sign of sickness.
  • DO use pet-safe disinfectants (diluted vinegar or chlorhexidine) rather than toxic aerosol or bleach cleaners.
  • DO quarantine new animal additions in a separate room for 30–60 days before introducing them to the general area.
  • DO maintain a backup power plan with battery aerators and heat packs for sudden winter power outages.
  • DO locate an emergency 24-hour exotic veterinarian before you ever need one in a crisis.

✖ Husbandry Don'ts

  • DON'T burn scented candles, incense, or spray aerosols in rooms where pets reside.
  • DON'T wait to seek veterinary care when a prey animal stops eating; digestive stasis can turn fatal within 24 hours.
  • DON'T use human medications, ointments, or antibiotics; many human analgesics (like acetaminophen or ibuprofen) are fatal.
  • DON'T house incompatible species together in the same enclosure.
  • DON'T rely on tap water without dechlorinating; municipal tap water contains lethal chloramines and heavy metals.
