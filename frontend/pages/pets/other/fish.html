01 Coldwater

Goldfish

Carassius auratus • Cyprinidae

One of the oldest domesticated fish in the world, goldfish are hardy, personable grazers with rich individual personalities. Contrary to the outdated "fishbowl" myth, they require spacious aquariums and heavy filtration to thrive.

📏 Size & Appearance

15–30 cm (6–12 inches). Broad spectrum of metallic orange, red, pearl white, calico, and velvet black. Body shapes range from streamlined single-tails (Comets, Shubunkins) to egg-shaped fancies (Fantails, Orandas).

Min Tank Size 20–30 Gal (Fancy) / 50+ Gal (Comet)
Water Temperature 18°C – 23°C (65°F – 74°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 7.0 – 8.0 • 8–18 dGH
Filtration Need Heavy (Bioload 5–8x turnover)
Behaviour: Peaceful bottom/mid grazer; highly social with similar goldfish.
Compatible: Other fancy goldfish, Bristlenose Plecos (in large cool tanks).
Avoid: Tropical fish (Bettas, Guppies, Tetras) & fin-nippers; temperature mismatch.
Care Tip: Goldfish lack true stomachs and produce substantial waste; weekly 25% water changes are vital.
02 Tropical Warm

Betta Fish

Betta splendens • Siamese Fighting Fish

Famous for their dramatic flowing fins and kaleidoscope of iridescent colors, Bettas possess a specialized labyrinth organ allowing them to breathe atmospheric air. They need a gently filtered, heated tank with soft plants.

📏 Size & Appearance

5–7 cm (2–2.8 inches). Renowned for spectacular tail variants including Crowntail, Halfmoon, Plakat, and Dumbo-ear, exhibiting metallic blues, crimson reds, royal purples, and pastels.

Min Tank Size 5 Gallons (19 L) minimum
Water Temperature 25°C – 28°C (76°F – 82°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 6.5 – 7.5 • 3–10 dGH
Filtration Need Low / Gentle flow (Sponge filter)
Behaviour: Territorial solitary centerpiece; surface dweller; builds bubble nests.
Compatible: Nerite snails, Amano shrimp, Corydoras catfish, peaceful tetras (10+ gal).
Avoid: Other male bettas, fancy guppies (mistaken for rivals), fin-nipping barbs.
Care Tip: Bowls are inadequate; bettas require a thermostat heater and low water flow to prevent fin rot.
03 Tropical Community

Guppy Fish

Poecilia reticulata • Millionfish

One of the most celebrated freshwater fish in history. Guppies are endlessly energetic, colorful livebearers that bring nonstop movement and excitement to beginner and community aquariums.

📏 Size & Appearance

Males 3–4 cm, Females 4–6 cm. Males display dazzling delta, sword, and veil tails with leopard spots, neon mosaics, cobras, and solid neons. Females are larger and more subdued.

Min Tank Size 10 Gallons (38 L)
Water Temperature 22°C – 28°C (72°F – 82°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 7.0 – 8.2 • 10–25 dGH (Hard water)
Filtration Need Gentle to moderate flow
Behaviour: Active, inquisitive, upper-column swimmer; highly social community fish.
Compatible: Platies, Mollies, Neon Tetras, Zebra Danios, Corydoras catfish, snails.
Avoid: Large predatory cichlids, aggressive fin-nippers (Tiger Barbs), aggressive bettas.
Care Tip: Keep 1 male to 2–3 females to reduce stress, or maintain all-male groups to prevent endless fry.
04 Tropical Community

Molly Fish

Poecilia sphenops / P. latipinna

Mollies are robust, adaptable livebearers cherished for their appetite for natural algae and friendly disposition. They thrive particularly well in hard, mineral-rich water and even light brackish setups.

📏 Size & Appearance

7.5–12 cm (3–5 inches). Deep, rectangular bodies with broad dorsal fins (and impressive sailfin varieties). Color strains include Black Mollies, Dalmatian, Marble, Golden, and Lyretail.

Min Tank Size 20 Gallons (75 L)
Water Temperature 24°C – 28°C (75°F – 82°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 7.5 – 8.5 • 15–30 dGH (Hard water)
Filtration Need Moderate to high oxygenation
Behaviour: Active, peaceful grazer; naturally nibbles green algae from plants and decor.
Compatible: Platies, Guppies, Swordtails, Angelfish (in 30+ gal), Corydoras, Snails.
Avoid: Coldwater goldfish, obligate soft/acidic water species, fin-nippers.
Care Tip: Include plant matter and spirulina flakes in their diet to satisfy their natural herbivorous grazing.
05 Tropical Schooling

Neon Tetra

Paracheirodon innesi • Characidae

Originating from the blackwater streams of the Amazon basin, the Neon Tetra is an aquarium icon. Their vivid, light-reflecting horizontal stripes create a mesmerizing kinetic spectacle when swimming in cohesive schools.

📏 Size & Appearance

3–4 cm (1.2–1.5 inches). An electric cyan-blue horizontal stripe glows from nose to adipose fin, paired with a vibrant crimson-red stripe extending from mid-body through the caudal fin.

Min Tank Size 10 Gallons (38 L)
Water Temperature 21°C – 26°C (70°F – 79°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 6.0 – 7.0 • 2–10 dGH (Soft water)
Filtration Need Gentle flow with live plant cover
Behaviour: Tightly schooling, peaceful mid-dweller; requires groups of 6–10+ minimum.
Compatible: Harlequin Rasboras, Corydoras, Otocinclus, Platies, Dwarf Gourami, Bettas.
Avoid: Adult Angelfish (they prey on neons!), large cichlids, aggressive barbs.
Care Tip: Keep in a school of at least 6–8 fish; solitary tetras become frightened, pale, and stressed.
06 Tropical Centerpiece

Freshwater Angelfish

Pterophyllum scalare • Cichlidae

With their regal disc-like bodies and sweeping wing-shaped fins, Angelfish are the undisputed monarchs of South American community aquariums. They are intelligent cichlids that quickly recognize their caregivers.

📏 Size & Appearance

15 cm body length, reaching 20–25 cm (8–10 inches) tall including fins! Laterally compressed disc bodies with elongated dorsal, anal, and filiform pelvic fins. Strains: Silver, Koi, Zebra, Marble, and Black.

Min Tank Size 30 Gal Tall (115 L) / 55+ Gal (Group)
Water Temperature 24°C – 29°C (76°F – 84°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 6.5 – 7.5 • 3–12 dGH
Filtration Need Moderate flow; tall tanks (18"+ height)
Behaviour: Graceful, semi-aggressive cichlid; territorial during spawning; forms bonded pairs.
Compatible: Medium-to-large tetras (Bleeding Heart, Lemon), Gouramis, Corydoras, Plecos.
Avoid: Tiny micro-fish (Neon Tetras get eaten!), Tiger Barbs (fin-nippers), Bettas.
Care Tip: Tall tanks are essential; standard shallow aquariums stunt their magnificent vertical fin development.
07 Tropical Community

Platy Fish

Xiphophorus maculatus • Poeciliidae

Widely considered the ultimate beginner fish, Platies are peaceful, exceptionally resilient, and full of charm. They accept almost all water conditions within reason and interact peacefully with all fellow tankmates.

📏 Size & Appearance

5–7 cm (2–2.8 inches). Sturdy, compact bodies with rounded fins. Available in hundreds of striking captive-bred color morphs including Red Wagtail, Mickey Mouse, Sunset, Blue Coral, and Tuxedo.

Min Tank Size 10–15 Gallons (38–57 L)
Water Temperature 22°C – 27°C (72°F – 80°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 7.0 – 8.3 • 10–25 dGH (Hard water)
Filtration Need Standard internal / Hang-on-back
Behaviour: Active, gentle, curious; explores all levels of the aquarium without aggression.
Compatible: Guppies, Mollies, Swordtails, Zebra Danios, Neon Tetras, Corydoras, Snails.
Avoid: Aggressive predatory cichlids, fin-nippers, coldwater goldfish.
Care Tip: Platies relish nibbling on live plants and algae wafers alongside their core staple flake food.
08 Subtropical / Tropical

Zebra Danio

Danio rerio • Zebrafish

Unstoppable energy in a compact package! Zebra Danios are bullet-fast schooling fish that swim playfully against filter currents. Their legendary hardiness makes them ideal for newcomers cycling a modern freshwater setup.

📏 Size & Appearance

4–5 cm (1.5–2 inches). Slender, streamlined torpedo silhouette adorned with 5 horizontal metallic blue and golden-silver racing stripes extending cleanly through the anal and tail fins.

Min Tank Size 10–15 Gallons Long (needs length)
Water Temperature 18°C – 24°C (64°F – 75°F)
Water pH & Hardness pH 6.5 – 7.8 • 5–15 dGH
Filtration Need Moderate to brisk current
Behaviour: Hyperactive, playful surface/mid darter; strictly schooling (keep 6–8+).
Compatible: Platies, Mollies, larger tetras, Corydoras catfish, Bristlenose Plecos.
Avoid: Slow, calm long-finned fish (Bettas) who become stressed by danio hyperactivity.
Care Tip: Use a tight-fitting aquarium lid; Danios are athletic surface jumpers when excited.

Foundation of Success

Aquarium Setup Basics

A thriving aquatic pet habitat relies on balanced biological filtration, proper positioning, and patience during the initial cycling phase.

01

Tank Selection & Placement

Place your aquarium on a level, sturdy stand away from direct sunlight (which triggers runaway algae) and cold air drafts. Remember: 10 gallons of water weighs ~85 lbs (38 kg)!

02

Substrate & Hardscape

Rinse inert aquarium gravel or fine river sand thoroughly. Add smooth driftwood, non-calcareous rocks, and ceramic caves for resting territories. Avoid sharp plastic decorations that tear fins.

03

Filtration & Aeration

Install a filter rated for at least 4 to 5 times your tank volume per hour. Sponge filters are superb for Bettas and small fry, while Hang-on-Back (HOB) filters excel in community tanks.

04

Heating & Lighting

Tropical fish require a submersible heater (3–5 watts per gallon) with a reliable thermometer. Set aquarium LED lights on an automatic timer for 6 to 8 hours daily to nourish plants without fostering algae.

The #1 Rule of Fishkeeping

Understanding the Nitrogen Cycle

Before introducing fish, beneficial bacteria must colonize the filter media to convert toxic fish wastes into harmless compounds. Never add a full load of fish on Day 1!

🐟 ➔ 💩 1. Waste & Ammonia Fish respiration, uneaten food, and waste create toxic Ammonia (NH₃). Lethal to fish even in tiny amounts.
🦠 2. Nitrosomonas Bacteria Beneficial nitrifying bacteria consume Ammonia and oxidize it into Nitrite (NO₂⁻). Still toxic to fish blood.
🌿 3. Nitrobacter Bacteria A second colony of bacteria converts Nitrite into Nitrate (NO₃⁻), which is far less harmful in low concentrations.
🪣 ➔ 🪴 4. Water Changes & Plants Weekly 20–25% partial water changes and live plants export Nitrate from the ecosystem, completing the cycle.
How to Cycle Your Tank: Perform a "fishless cycle" using a small pinch of fish food or pure household ammonia and bottled nitrifying bacteria. Test water every few days until Ammonia and Nitrite drop to 0 ppm and Nitrates register, typically taking 2 to 4 weeks.
Water Chemistry

Water Quality & Testing Standards

Fish live immersed in their environment; pristine, chemically stable water is the single most important factor for disease prevention and longevity.

Ammonia (NH₃)

Must Be 0.0 ppm

Produced by gills and waste. Highly toxic at any detectable level; burns fish gills and causes gasping at the surface.

Fix: Immediate 30–50% water change with dechlorinator.

Nitrite (NO₂⁻)

Must Be 0.0 ppm

Intermediate byproduct of the nitrogen cycle. Binds to hemoglobin, suffocating fish internally ("brown blood disease").

Fix: Test filter biological media; dose beneficial bacteria.

Nitrate (NO₃⁻)

< 20–40 ppm

Relatively non-toxic final product of biological filtration. Becomes stressful to fish immune systems when accumulating over 40 ppm.

Fix: Perform routine weekly 20–25% partial water changes.

pH Level

6.5 – 8.0 (Stable)

Measures acidity or alkalinity. Stability is much more critical than matching an exact decimal point; wild pH swings induce fatal osmotic shock.

Tip: Avoid chemical pH adjusters; use natural driftwood or crushed coral.

Temperature

Species Specific

Tropical species require 24°C–28°C, while goldfish thrive at 18°C–22°C. Fluctuations greater than 1°C per day stress fish and weaken immune defenses.

Tip: Always check digital heater status daily.

Chlorine & Chloramine

Must Be 0.0 ppm

Disinfectants added to municipal tap water. Lethal to fish and instantly destroys the beneficial bacteria in your biological filter.

Rule: ALWAYS dose tap water with dechlorinator before adding to tank!

Daily Husbandry

Basic Fish Care & Wellness Tips

Simple daily habits prevent the vast majority of aquarium problems. Follow these core care principles for vibrant, long-lived aquatic companions.

🥣

Feeding Discipline: Avoid Overfeeding

A fish's stomach is approximately the size of its eyeball. Feed only what your fish can consume completely within 90 to 120 seconds, once or twice daily. Uneaten food rots quickly, triggering catastrophic ammonia spikes. Fast your fish one day a week to keep digestive systems clear.

🔍

Daily 5-Minute Observation

Spend five quiet minutes watching your tank every day during feeding. Observe swim patterns, check that all fish have healthy appetites, verify fins are fully open (not clamped), and confirm the heater and filter are running smoothly. Early detection saves lives.

🩺

Disease Prevention & Common Ailments

Keep an eye out for Ich (White Spot) (looks like grains of salt sprinkled on scales), Fin Rot (frayed, milky fin edges caused by bacteria in dirty water), and Swim Bladder Disorder. Maintaining clean, warm, stable water is 90% of the cure.

🛡️

The Quarantine Tank (QT) Protocol

Always quarantine newly purchased fish in a small, separate 5–10 gallon tank for 2 to 3 weeks before adding them to your main display aquarium. This simple precaution ensures hidden illnesses or parasites do not spread to your established community.

Aquarium Care FAQs

Clear answers to the most common questions asked by new aquarium hobbyists.

Unfiltered glass bowls offer very little surface area for gas exchange, hold too little volume to dilute ammonia, and cannot accommodate a reliable filter or heater. Bettas require at least 5 gallons with a heater set to 26°C, and Goldfish require 20–30+ gallons with heavy filtration due to their rapid growth and substantial waste production.
You should wait 2 to 4 weeks while completing a full fishless nitrogen cycle. Simply letting a tank run with water and filter for 24 hours does NOT cycle it; nitrifying bacteria require an ammonia source (fish food or bottled ammonia) to establish colonies in your filter media before fish can safely survive.
In a newly set up aquarium, milky cloudy water is typically a harmless bacterial bloom. It occurs as microscopic heterotrophic bacteria rapidly multiply in the water column while the biological ecosystem is finding its balance. Do not panic or do massive 100% water changes; it usually clears naturally on its own in 3 to 7 days as the biological filter matures.
Clean filter media once every 3 to 4 weeks. Crucial rule: Never rinse filter sponges or bio-rings under chlorinated tap water, as chlorine instantly kills the beneficial nitrifying bacteria. Always gently squeeze and rinse filter media in a bucket of siphoned tank water during your regular water change.
